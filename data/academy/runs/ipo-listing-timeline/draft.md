# From close to listing: the T+3 timeline

When an IPO closes, your money is already blocked in your bank account. The shares are not yet yours. What happens in the three working days between that closing date and the day you can trade the shares is a tightly scheduled sequence of checks, transfers, and approvals — all compressed into a timeline that SEBI shortened from six working days to three in August 2023.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

The rule is called T+3. T is the issue closing date — the last day you could bid. T+1 is the first working day after that, T+2 the second, T+3 the third. If an IPO closes on a Monday, and there are no market holidays in between, the shares start trading on Thursday. The T+3 timeline must be disclosed in the offer document.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

This article walks through each of those three days: what happens, when it happens, and what it means for your blocked funds and your eventual share credit.

---

## Day T+1: Basis of allotment and PAN verification

The first working day after the IPO closes is the day the registrar and the stock exchange decide who gets shares.

The registrar — the firm appointed by the issuer to manage the application process — takes every valid application and runs it through two checks. The first is a PAN match. For UPI applications, the registrar must complete this third-party verification — matching the PAN on the application with the PAN linked to the bank account and the PAN linked to the demat account — by 9:30 am on T+1. For non-UPI applications, the deadline is 1 pm on T+1.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline] If the PANs do not match, the application is treated as invalid. No shares will be allotted, no matter how many lots you bid for.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

The second check is the basis of allotment itself. For an oversubscribed issue, this is where the lottery happens: the registrar determines how many shares each successful applicant gets, following the allocation rules for each investor category. The basis of allotment must be finalised before 6 pm on T+1.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

The stock exchange then reviews and approves the basis. That approval must come before 9 pm on T+1.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline] Once the exchange signs off, the allotment is locked. The registrar knows exactly who gets shares and who does not.

| Activity on T+1 | Deadline |
|---|---|
| PAN third-party verification — UPI applications | 9:30 am |
| PAN third-party verification — non-UPI applications | 1:00 pm |
| Finalisation of basis of allotment | 6:00 pm |
| Stock exchange approval of basis | 9:00 pm |

*Source: SEBI Master Circular for ICDR, Annexure XXIII*[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

## Day T+2: Money moves and shares credit

The second working day is when the money and the shares actually move.

By 9:30 am on T+2, the registrar initiates fund transfer instructions. Two things happen simultaneously. For applicants who were allotted shares, the full application amount is debited from their bank accounts — the blocked funds are finally taken. For applicants who received no allotment, the blocked amount is unblocked — released back for use. The debiting of allottee accounts must be completed by 2 pm on T+2. The unblocking of non-allottee funds must be completed by 4 pm on T+2.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

At the same time, the depository — NSDL or CDSL — is working on the share credit. The corporate action for crediting shares to the demat accounts of allottees is initiated before 2 pm on T+2 and must be completed before 6 pm on T+2.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline] By the end of the day, every allottee should see the shares sitting in their demat account.

The issuer also files the listing application with the stock exchanges on T+2. The exchanges issue a trading notice — the formal announcement that the shares will begin trading — before 7:30 pm on T+2. And the allotment advertisement, which lists the basis of allotment and the number of applicants in each category, appears on the websites of the issuer, the registrar, and the exchanges before 9 pm on T+2.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

## Day T+3: Trading begins

The third working day is the day the shares hit the market.

Trading starts on T+3. This is the first day investors can buy or sell the newly listed shares on the stock exchange.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

The allotment advertisement, which was published on websites on T+2, appears in newspapers on T+3, or at the latest on T+4.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

By this point, the process is complete. Allottees have shares in their demat accounts and the blocked amount has been debited. Non-allottees have their funds unblocked, available for their next use.

## What if my funds are not unblocked on time?

The deadlines for unblocking are strict, and SEBI has built a compensation mechanism for when they are missed.

For an applicant who receives no allotment at all, the blocked amount must be unblocked by T+2 — the second working day after the issue closes. For an applicant who receives a partial allotment — say you bid for three lots but got only one — the excess amount (the portion not allotted) must be unblocked by the next working day after the basis of allotment is finalised. Since the basis is finalised on T+1, that next working day is T+2.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

If the bank or the intermediary — the self-certified syndicate bank (SCSB) or the sponsor bank in the UPI flow — fails to unblock by these deadlines, compensation to the investor is computed from T+3 day onwards.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

| Scenario | Unblocking deadline | Compensation starts from |
|---|---|---|
| No allotment | T+2 | T+3 |
| Partial allotment (excess amount) | T+2 (next working day after T+1 basis finalisation) | T+3 |

*Source: SEBI Master Circular for ICDR, Chapter 11*[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]

The circular that sets the T+3 timeline fixes only that the counting of any such delay starts from the T+3 day.[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline] Investors should check the offer document for the specific penalty terms applicable to the issue.

---

The T+3 timeline is a significant compression from the earlier T+6 standard. It means your money is blocked for fewer days, your shares arrive faster, and the gap between applying and trading has shrunk from a full week to three working days. The sequence — PAN checks on T+1, money movement and demat credit on T+2, trading on T+3 — is now the standard rhythm of every Indian public issue.

[^sebi-master-circular-asba-upi-timeline]: SEBI Master Circular for ICDR, November 11, 2024, Chapter 11 and Annexure XXIII. The T+3 timeline was introduced via SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/TPD1/CIR/P/2023/140 dated August 9, 2023.
