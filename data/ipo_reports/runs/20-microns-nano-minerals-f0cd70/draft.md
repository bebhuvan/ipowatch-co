# 20 Microns Nano Minerals: Inside the DRHP

20 Microns Nano Minerals Limited processes and sells specialty chemicals — functional additives and chemically modified minerals that improve the durability, texture, and colour of everyday products like paints, plastics, and cosmetics. The company is part of the larger 20 Microns group, and its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, markets and sells its products through an established network. Now the company is raising capital through an IPO to fund a new manufacturing facility in Vadodara, Gujarat, and to list its shares on NSE EMERGE, the SME platform for smaller companies. [^drhp-p75] [^drhp-p112] [^drhp-p113]

The offer is being managed by Vivro Financial Services Private Limited as the lead manager, with Link Intime India Private Limited acting as the registrar. [^drhp-p54] [^drhp-p55]

## What the company does

The company's product range falls into four categories: Functional Additives (FA), Chemically Modified Minerals (CMM), Soft Minerals (SM), and Hard Minerals (HM). These are specialty chemicals — substances added to other materials to change their properties. A paint manufacturer, for instance, might use a functional additive to make its product more durable or to give it a specific texture. A plastics company might use a chemically modified mineral to improve heat resistance or colour consistency. [^drhp-p112]

The company does not sell directly to end-users in most cases. Instead, it has a long-term arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, which markets and sells the company's products through its own network. This arrangement means the company's revenue depends significantly on the promoter's distribution capability. [^drhp-p112] [^drhp-p113]

Customer concentration is moderate but notable. The top 10 customers accounted for about 59% of revenue from operations in each of FY2015, FY2016, and FY2017. [^drhp-p128] This means the loss of any single large customer could have a material impact on revenue.

The company also exports, though the volumes are modest. Over five years, cumulative exports totalled ₹1,068.18 lakh, going to Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, and Sri Lanka. [^drhp-p128]

The company lists six competitive strengths in its filing: a diversified product portfolio, diversified user industries, import substitution, support from its holding company, product innovation and continuous R&D, and a strong management team. [^drhp-p113] [^drhp-p45] These are standard claims for a specialty chemicals company, but the diversification across industries — paints, plastics, cosmetics, construction — does provide some buffer against a downturn in any single end-market.

## Industry and market

The Indian specialty chemicals market (excluding agrochemicals and dyestuff) was estimated at approximately ₹1,000 billion in FY2017, according to CRISIL Research cited in the DRHP. [^drhp-p92] The broader Indian chemicals industry (excluding pharmaceuticals and fertilisers) was estimated at ₹5,800–6,200 billion in the same period. [^drhp-p92]

The specialty chemicals segment grew at a CAGR of 10–12% from FY2014 to FY2017 and is forecast to grow at 12–14% CAGR over the next few years. [^drhp-p94] Within this broad segment, growth rates vary by sub-segment: construction chemicals and water chemicals are each expected to grow at 15% CAGR, while polymer additives are expected to grow at 10% CAGR over the next three years. [^drhp-p94]

The industry is highly fragmented, with small and medium enterprises holding 70–75% market share. [^drhp-p94] Global players also compete, but only a few Indian players have the scale to match them in product development. [^drhp-p95] This fragmentation means that a company like 20 Microns Nano Minerals competes against both large multinationals and hundreds of smaller domestic players.

Key demand drivers cited in the filing include increased consumption intensity, rising middle-class households (projected to reach 148 million by 2030), and government initiatives such as the Draft National Chemical Policy and 100% FDI allowance in the sector. [^drhp-p95] [^drhp-p96] The government has also approved four Petroleum, Chemicals and Petrochemicals Investment Regions (PCPIRs) — in Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Odisha — with total proposed investments of about ₹9 trillion. ## Key risks

### 1. No long-term contracts with customers or suppliers

The company has not entered into long-term or definitive agreements with its customers, relying instead on purchase orders to govern the volume, pricing, and other terms of sales. Similarly, it has no long-term agreements with raw material suppliers. This creates vulnerability to volume and price fluctuations — a customer could reduce orders or demand lower prices with little notice, and the company has no contractual protection against raw material cost increases. [^drhp-p25] [^drhp-p26]

### 2. Negative cash flows from operations

The company reported negative cash flows from operations of ₹349.75 lakh in FY2017 — a year when it reported a profit of ₹174.82 lakh. This divergence between profit and cash flow suggests that the company may be funding working capital growth or facing collection issues. Sustained negative cash flows could strain liquidity and force the company to rely on external financing. [^drhp-p22]

### 3. Regulatory non-compliance at a manufacturing facility

It also has a history of delayed or incorrect filings with the RoC. Penalties or enforcement actions could disrupt operations or result in financial penalties. [^drhp-p18] [^drhp-p19]

### 4. Intense competition in a fragmented industry
