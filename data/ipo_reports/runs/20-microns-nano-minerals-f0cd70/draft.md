# 20 Microns Nano Minerals: inside the DRHP

20 Microns Nano Minerals Limited processes and sells specialty chemicals — functional additives and chemically modified minerals that go into paints, plastics, cosmetics, and construction materials. The company does not own the mines that produce the raw minerals; it buys them, processes them into finer or chemically altered forms, and sells them to industrial customers. What makes the business model distinctive — and risky — is that it relies on its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, to market and sell its products through the promoter's own distribution network.[^drhp-p112]

The company is raising capital through an SME IPO on NSE EMERGE, the exchange's platform for smaller companies.[^drhp-p75] The issue is entirely a fresh issue of equity shares — no existing shareholders are selling. The proceeds are earmarked for capital expenditure on a new manufacturing facility at Plot 336, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat, and for general corporate purposes.[^drhp-p75] The price band has not yet been set in the DRHP excerpts. As of September 30, 2017, the company had an issued, subscribed and paid-up capital of ₹897.00 lakh (89,70,000 equity shares of ₹10 each).[^drhp-p190]

The promoters — Mr. Chandresh S. Parikh, Mr. Atil C. Parikh, Mr. Rajesh C. ## What the company does

The company's product range falls into four categories: Functional Additives (FA), Chemically Modified Minerals (CMM), Soft Minerals (SM), and Hard Minerals (HM).[^drhp-p112] These are inputs for industries as varied as paints and coatings, plastics, rubber, adhesives, cosmetics, paper, and construction. The company does not manufacture the minerals themselves; it processes them — grinding, surface-treating, chemically modifying — to improve their performance in specific end-uses.

The most important structural feature of the business is its long-term arrangement with 20 Microns Limited, the corporate promoter. The company markets and sells its products through the promoter's network.[^drhp-p112] This is not a typical arm's-length distributor relationship; the DRHP describes it as a source of "substantial synergies."[^drhp-p113] It is also a dependency: if that arrangement were disrupted, the company would need to build its own sales and marketing infrastructure from scratch.

Customer concentration is moderate but worth noting. The top 10 customers accounted for around 59%, 57%, and 59% of revenue from operations in FY2015, FY2016, and FY2017 respectively.[^drhp-p128] That is not extreme — many industrial companies have higher concentration — but it means the loss of a single large customer could be felt.

Geographically, the company has exported to Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, and Sri Lanka, with total exports of ₹1,068.18 lakh over the past five years.[^drhp-p128] The filing does not break out export revenue as a percentage of total revenue.

The DRHP lists five competitive strengths: a diversified product portfolio, diversified user industries, import substitution potential, support from the holding company, and product innovation through continuous R&D.[^drhp-p113][^drhp-p45] These are standard claims in a filing, but the import-substitution angle is worth noting: if Indian manufacturers are replacing imported specialty chemicals with domestic alternatives, a company that can match quality and price stands to benefit.

## Industry and market

The specialty chemicals segment in India — excluding agrochemicals and dyestuff — was estimated at roughly ₹1,000 billion in fiscal 2017.[^drhp-p92] The broader Indian chemicals industry (excluding pharmaceuticals and fertilisers) was estimated at ₹5,800–6,200 billion in the same period.[^drhp-p92] Specialty chemicals accounted for about 17% of that total.

CRISIL Research estimated that the specialty chemicals segment grew at 10–12% CAGR from fiscal 2014 to 2017 and is forecast to grow at 12–14% CAGR going forward.[^drhp-p94] Within the segment, growth rates vary by sub-segment: construction chemicals and water chemicals are projected to grow at 15% CAGR, polymer additives at 10% CAGR, textiles at 12% CAGR, and personal care ingredients at 15% CAGR.[^drhp-p94]

The industry is highly fragmented. Small and medium enterprises hold 70–75% market share, and only a few Indian players have the scale or capability to compete with global giants on product development and innovation.[^drhp-p94][^drhp-p95] That fragmentation creates both opportunity and risk: a well-run SME can carve out a defensible niche, but pricing pressure from larger competitors is constant.

Key demand drivers cited in the DRHP include rising consumption, improved living standards, government initiatives (a Draft National Chemical Policy aimed at increasing the chemicals sector's share of GDP, and 100% FDI allowed in the sector), and favourable global shifts — particularly China's stricter environmental regulations, which have reduced its specialty chemical output and opened space for Indian producers.[^drhp-p95][^drhp-p96]

Regulatory context matters for any chemical company. Exports to Europe are governed by the REACH regime (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals).[^drhp-p110] Domestically, GST came into effect from July 1, 2017, replacing multiple indirect taxes with a single tax.[^drhp-p111] The government has also approved four Petroleum, Chemicals and Petrochemicals Investment Regions (PCPIRs) in Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Odisha, with total proposed investments of about ₹9 trillion.[^drhp-p111]

## Key risks from the filing

### 1. No long-term agreements with customers or suppliers

The company operates entirely on purchase orders. It has no long-term or definitive agreements with customers governing volume, pricing, or other terms of sale.[^drhp-p26] Similarly, it has no long-term agreements with raw material suppliers, exposing it to availability and price shocks.[^drhp-p25] In a business where raw material costs are a major input, this is a structural vulnerability.

### 2. Dependence on the corporate promoter for marketing and sales

The long-term arrangement with 20 Microns Limited is not a convenience; it is the company's primary route to market. The DRHP describes it as a source of "substantial synergies,"[^drhp-p113] but it is also a single point of failure. If the arrangement were terminated or disrupted, the company would need to build its own sales network — a costly and time-consuming process.

### 3. Negative operating cash flow in FY2014 and volatility since

The company burned ₹(349.75) lakh in operating cash in FY2014.[^drhp-p50] While it has been cash-positive from operations in every subsequent period, the volatility is notable: from ₹802.59 lakh in FY2015 to ₹127.73 lakh in FY2017. The DRHP itself flags that sustained negative cash flow could impact growth and business.[^drhp-p22]

### 4. Missing statutory licenses at one manufacturing facility

The company operates a facility at Plot 9–10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat without having obtained certain required licenses. The DRHP acknowledges the risk of penalties.[^drhp-p19] This is not a theoretical concern; regulatory action could disrupt production at that facility.

### 5. Contingent liabilities of ₹29.11 lakh

As of September 30, 2017, the company had contingent liabilities and commitments aggregating to ₹29.11 lakh.[^drhp-p21] While not large in absolute terms — roughly 17% of the FY2017 PAT — these could materialise and affect financial condition.
