## What the company does

The business is mineral processing. 20 Microns Nano Minerals takes raw minerals and turns them into specialty chemicals — products engineered for a specific function rather than sold as generic commodities. Its portfolio spans four categories: Functional Additives (FA), Chemically Modified Minerals (CMM), Soft Minerals (SM) and Hard Minerals (HM).[^drhp-p112] The company lists a diversified product portfolio and diversified user industries among its competitive strengths, along with import substitution, product innovation through continuous R&D, a strong management team, and the support of its holding company.[^drhp-p113][^drhp-p45]

The sales model is the through-line. The company has a long-term arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited,[^drhp-p113] to market and sell its products through the promoter's network.[^drhp-p112] The company says it derives substantial synergies from this arrangement.[^drhp-p113] What that means in practice: the entity that controls the company is also its sales force. The company's revenue depends on a related party performing the marketing function.

The physical footprint is modest. Manufacturing happens at two locations — Plot 347, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat, and Plot 104/3, Thenkasi Road, Pathur Village, Tirunelveli, Tamil Nadu — with administrative offices in Bhuj and Udaipur.[^drhp-p20] The new capital expenditure is planned at Plot 336, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara.[^drhp-p75]

Two structural facts about how the business operates deserve attention. First, sales are not contracted. The company has no long-term or definitive agreements with customers; volume, pricing and other terms are governed by purchase orders, order by order.[^drhp-p26] Second, raw material purchases sit on the same footing — no long-term agreements with suppliers, so timely supply at competitive prices is not assured.[^drhp-p25] The company is, in effect, a spot-market operator on both sides of its business, with its promoter as the distribution channel in between.

Customer concentration is high. The top 10 customers accounted for around 59% of revenue from operations in FY15, 57% in FY16, and 59% in FY17.[^drhp-p128] Exports over the past five years totalled ₹10.68 crore, going to Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Sri Lanka.[^drhp-p128]

## Industry: specialty chemicals

The company operates in India's specialty chemicals market — the segment of the chemicals industry that sells function-specific products rather than bulk commodities. CRISIL Research, cited in the filing, put this market at roughly ₹1,000 billion in fiscal 2017, about 17% of the Indian chemicals industry, which it estimated at ₹5,800–6,200 billion.[^drhp-p92] The segment grew at 10–12% CAGR from fiscal 2014 to 2017, and CRISIL forecasts 12–14% CAGR ahead.[^drhp-p94]

The market structure matters for a company this size. The industry is highly fragmented, with SMEs holding 70–75% market share; only a few Indian players have the scale or capability to compete with global giants on product development and innovation.[^drhp-p94][^drhp-p95] The filing names the growth drivers: increased intensity of consumption, improved consumption standards, end-use demand from a middle class that could reach 148 million households by 2030, and government support including 100% FDI approval and a Draft National Chemical Policy.[^drhp-p95][^drhp-p96]

The headwinds are equally specific. India's chemicals industry has imported more than it exported every year from FY13 to FY17, with imports growing at 6.4% CAGR versus 6% for exports.[^drhp-p92] Exports to the European Union face REACH — the Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals regulation — a strict compliance regime.[^drhp-p110] Downstream demand in plastics is strong: CRISIL expects polymer demand to grow at 9–10% CAGR and plastic products at 11–13% CAGR between fiscals 2017 and 2020.[^drhp-p103]

## Five material risks

**1. No long-term contracts on either side of the business.** Sales run on purchase orders; raw material supply has no long-term agreements. Volume, price and margins can move with every order, and the company cannot assure timely raw material supply at competitive prices.[^drhp-p26][^drhp-p25]

**2. Customer concentration without contractual protection.** The top 10 customers supplied roughly 59% of revenue in each of the last three full years.[^drhp-p128] Because there are no long-term agreements, losing one large buyer would hit the top line immediately and without notice.[^drhp-p26]

**3. The promoter is the sales channel.** Revenue depends on a long-term arrangement under which corporate promoter 20 Microns Limited[^drhp-p113] markets and sells the company's products through its network.[^drhp-p112] The company calls this a strength; it is also a dependency. If the promoter's network underperforms, the company has no independent distribution of its own.

**4. Regulatory gaps.** Not all statutory approvals for the Plot 9-10, GIDC Waghodia facility have been obtained, and the company cannot assure it will not be penalised for non-compliance.[^drhp-p19] Past RoC filings have been late or incorrect, exposing the company to penalties.[^drhp-p18]

Negative operating cash flow in a profitable year.** Operating cash flow was ₹3.50 crore negative in FY17 despite a profit of ₹1.75 crore.[^drhp-p50][^drhp-p49] The DRHP itself notes the company's negative cash flows in the restated financial statements.[^drhp-p22] A company that books profit but burns cash cannot fund its own expansion.
