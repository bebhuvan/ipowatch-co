## What the company does

Specialty chemicals are the opposite of commodity chemicals. A commodity chemical is bought on price; a specialty chemical is bought because it does a job: it makes a polymer resist heat, a coating adhere, a mineral disperse evenly in a liquid. 20 Microns Nano Minerals processes minerals into four product groups: Functional Additives, Chemically Modified Minerals, Soft Minerals and Hard Minerals.[^drhp-p112] The company lists a diversified product portfolio, diversified user industries and import substitution as competitive strengths, along with product innovation, continuous R&D, a strong management team, and the support of its holding company.[^drhp-p113][^drhp-p45]

Sales run through one channel: a long-term arrangement with the corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, to market and sell its products through the promoter's network.[^drhp-p112] The filing frames this as a strength (the company "derives substantial synergies" from the corporate promoter), but it is also a dependency.[^drhp-p113] If that relationship were to break down, the company would lose its primary channel to customers.

Sales are not secured by contracts. The company has no long-term or definitive agreements with its customers; volume, pricing and other terms are governed by purchase orders.[^drhp-p26] The same applies on the supply side: there are no long-term agreements with raw material suppliers, and the company's ability to obtain raw material in a timely manner, in sufficient quantities and at competitive prices is an identified risk.[^drhp-p25] This is a business that lives order to order, on both ends.

Customer concentration is high. The top ten customers accounted for around 59% of revenue from operations in FY2015, 57% in FY2016 and 59% in FY2017.[^drhp-p128] There is an export book, though a small one: about ₹1,068.18 lakh in total exports over the past five years, to Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Sri Lanka.[^drhp-p128]

## The market: a fragmented specialty chemicals sector growing at double digits

The market is large and growing. CRISIL Research estimated the Indian chemicals industry, excluding pharmaceuticals and fertilisers, at ₹5,800–6,200 billion in fiscal 2017; specialty chemicals, other than agrochemicals and dyestuff, accounted for roughly 17% of that, about ₹1,000 billion.[^drhp-p92] The specialty chemicals segment grew at a 10–12% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) from fiscal 2014 to 2017, and CRISIL forecasts 12–14% ahead.[^drhp-p94]

The fastest-growing segments within specialty chemicals are construction chemicals, water chemicals and personal care ingredients, each expected to grow at 15% CAGR; surfactants at 13%; textile chemicals and flavours-and-fragrances at 12%; and polymer additives at 10%.[^drhp-p94] The filing also cites demand for polymer growing at 9–10% CAGR between fiscals 2017 and 2020, and plastic products at 11–13%.[^drhp-p103]

The growth story rests on several drivers. The filing cites rising consumption intensity and improved consumption standards, along with an Indian middle class that could reach 148 million households by 2030.[^drhp-p95] The government has released a Draft National Chemical Policy aimed at increasing the chemicals sector's share of GDP, and foreign direct investment (FDI) of up to 100% is allowed in the sector.[^drhp-p95][^drhp-p96] There is also a global tailwind: China, a major player in commodity chemicals, has seen stricter environmental regulations since 2015 that have affected its chemical manufacturing output, shifting the global balance.[^drhp-p96]

The import-substitution argument has a statistical base. Over the five years from 2012-13 to 2016-17, chemicals imports grew at a 6.4% CAGR, slightly faster than exports at 6%.[^drhp-p92] The sector is highly fragmented: SMEs hold 70–75% of the market, and only a few Indian players have the scale or capability to compete with global giants on product development and innovation.[^drhp-p94][^drhp-p95]
