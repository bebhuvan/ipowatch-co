## What the company does

The infrastructure arm does three kinds of work. It designs and builds railway assets: platforms, station buildings, gauge conversion, and track laying. It takes on bridge contracts across beam, cantilever, arch, suspension, cable-stayed and truss bridges. And it builds bituminous and concrete roads connecting highways and district roads [^drhp-p71]. The concrete arm is a single facility, a ready-mix plant at Kashimira in Thane district, Maharashtra, which the filing describes as its advanced equipment and quality-control site [^drhp-p67].

How the work is won matters more than what the work is. Public construction contracts in India are awarded by tender, and the filing sets out the sequence in six steps. The client floats the tender for a specific job. A pre-bid meeting resolves queries. The contractor bids, taking into account whether it is eligible for that particular contract. The job is awarded to the lowest quote, and a letter of acceptance goes to the lowest bidder. The contractor then deposits a performance guarantee, drawings are issued, and the site is handed over [^drhp-p72].

There is no negotiation on price in this model. The lowest number wins. That single fact explains why the company's own SWOT analysis lists a working capital crunch as a threat to profitability: a contractor that has won on the thinnest possible margin then has to fund labour, materials and a performance guarantee before the client pays [^drhp-p76].

The client list is almost entirely public bodies. The Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai, Western Railway, Central Railway, Mumbai Railway Vikas Corporation Ltd, Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority and the Dedicated Freight Corridor Corporation of India Ltd all appear, alongside private names including Sky Way Infrastructure, Dev Engineers, GCC Hotel and Krypton Corp [^drhp-p63]. One name on that list is not like the others. Western Railway has been a client since 2006 and has been a major contributor to revenue [^drhp-p67]. It accounts for 52% of the orders on hand [^drhp-p65], and revenue from key customers for the year ended March 31, 2018 was 57% of total revenue [^drhp-p67].

Geographically the company describes itself as a major player in Mumbai, consolidating its position in Maharashtra [^drhp-p67]. It does not describe operations outside the state. One technical capability it puts forward as a strength is box pushing, a method of driving a concrete box under an existing railway line or road without cutting through the traffic above it [^drhp-p67]. That is the kind of technique that suits railway work in a dense city, where closing a line to build under it is expensive and causes delays.

## The five risks that matter most

One customer. The filing states that the majority of revenues will depend on Indian Railways, its largest customer, contributing 57% of revenue in Fiscal 2018 [^drhp-p12]. The order book points the same way: Western Railway alone is 52% of orders on hand [^drhp-p65]. A reader should treat this single relationship as the swing factor in the entire business.

The revenue model depends on winning bids, and the process awards to the lowest quote. The filing warns that revenues depend on acceptance of bids submitted to the government and government departments, that attempts to secure government and PSU projects may not always succeed, and that its financial condition would be materially and adversely affected if it fails to obtain new contracts [^drhp-p12] [^drhp-p72].

Cash flow and a demand loan. The filing discloses negative cash flow in recent financial years and warns that sustained negative cash flow could hurt the business. It also discloses ₹405.02 lakhs of unsecured loans outstanding as on March 31, 2018 that are repayable on demand, and says a call on them could affect cash flow and financial condition. Slow recovery from trade receivables is flagged separately [^drhp-p16].

Geography. Revenue is concentrated in Mumbai, and the filing warns that adverse developments affecting operations in that region could hurt revenue and results [^drhp-p13]. In a tender-driven business, a slowdown in one city's public works pipeline is not a diversifiable problem.

Cost overruns and order-book illusion. The filing warns that the company may see reduced profits or losses, or cancellations and deferrals, if contract costs rise above estimates, and states that contracts in the order book may be adjusted, cancelled or suspended by clients, so the order book is not necessarily indicative of future revenues or earnings [^drhp-p14].
