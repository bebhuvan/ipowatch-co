20 Microns Nano Minerals Limited processes and sells specialty chemicals — functional additives and chemically modified minerals — and relies on a long-term marketing arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, to reach customers. [^drhp-p112] The company is raising capital through a fresh issue of equity shares on the SME platform of NSE (NSE EMERGE), managed by Vivro Financial Services Private Limited, with proceeds earmarked for a new manufacturing facility at GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, and other corporate purposes. [^drhp-p54-75]

## What the company does

The company sells four categories of specialty chemicals: Functional Additives (FA), Chemically Modified Minerals (CMM), Soft Minerals (SM) and Hard Minerals (HM). [^drhp-p112] These are not commodity chemicals sold by the tonne to a single industry. They are tailored compounds — additives that change the properties of plastics, paints, adhesives, rubber — and they find use across many industrial customers. The company lists a diversified product portfolio and diversified user industries among its competitive strengths. [^drhp-p113]

The business model has a distinctive feature: the company does not market its own products directly. It has a long-term arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, to sell through the promoter's network. [^drhp-p112] The company describes this as support from its holding company. [^drhp-p113] It is also a dependency — the company's revenue depends on the promoter's distribution reach and willingness to allocate sales effort to its products.

Customer concentration is high. The top 10 customers accounted for 59% of revenue from operations in each of the last three fiscal years — 59% in FY2015, 57% in FY2016, and 59% in FY2017. [^drhp-p128] That means the loss of any one of these customers could have a material impact on revenue. The company does not name the customers in the DRHP excerpts, but the concentration is disclosed as a risk factor.

The export footprint is modest but real. Over the past five years, the company achieved total exports of ₹1,068.18 lakh, shipping to Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Sri Lanka. [^drhp-p128] The filing does not break out exports as a percentage of revenue, so the relative importance of these markets is not clear from the disclosed data.

## Industry and market

The Indian specialty chemicals segment — excluding agrochemicals and dyestuff — was estimated at ₹1,443 billion in fiscal 2017, having grown at 10-12% CAGR from fiscal 2014 to 2017. [^drhp-p94] CRISIL Research forecasts the segment to grow at 12-14% CAGR. [^drhp-p94] Within this broad category, several sub-segments relevant to the company's product range are expected to grow faster: construction chemicals, water chemicals and personal care ingredients are each forecast to grow at 15% CAGR over the next three to five years. [^drhp-p94]

The industry is fragmented. Small and medium enterprises hold 70-75% market share, and only a few Indian players have the scale to compete with global giants on product development and innovation. [^drhp-p94-95] This fragmentation works both ways: it means the company faces many small competitors, but it also means no single player dominates the market.

Several structural tailwinds support the industry. Rising middle-class consumption and 100% FDI allowance in chemicals are cited as growth drivers. [^drhp-p95-96] The Government of India has released a Draft National Chemical Policy aimed at increasing the chemicals sector's share of GDP. [^drhp-p95] Import substitution is a stated opportunity: India's chemicals imports (growing at 6.4% CAGR) have outpaced exports (6% CAGR) over the five years to 2016-17, meaning domestic producers have room to replace foreign supply. [^drhp-p92]

## The offer structure

The IPO is a fresh issue of equity shares — there is no offer for sale by existing shareholders. The lead manager to the issue is Vivro Financial Services Private Limited, and the registrar is Link Intime India Private Limited. [^drhp-p54-55]

The objects of the issue are stated clearly. Proceeds will be used to (1) fund capital expenditure for setting up a new manufacturing facility at Plot 336, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat; (2) general corporate purposes; and (3) create a public trading market for the company's equity shares by listing on NSE EMERGE, which the company says will enhance its visibility and brand. [^drhp-p75]

The third objective is worth pausing on. Listing on the SME platform is not just about raising capital. It creates a public market for shares that were previously held almost entirely by promoters. That liquidity has value for the promoters themselves, who hold 99.76% of the pre-issue capital. [^drhp-p155]

## Financial performance

The restated financial statements cover five full fiscal years (FY2013 through FY2017) and the six-month period ended September 30, 2017. [^drhp-p49] All figures are in ₹ lakh.

**Profit after tax** shows a clear turnaround. The company was loss-making in FY2013 and FY2014, with losses of ₹27.40 lakh and ₹12.17 lakh respectively. [^drhp-p49] It turned profitable in FY2015, posting a PAT of ₹149.58 lakh. [^drhp-p49] Profitability has been sustained since: ₹90.18 lakh in FY2016, ₹174.82 lakh in FY2017, and ₹132.75 lakh in the six months ended September 2017. [^drhp-p49] The FY2017 figure is the highest full-year profit in the disclosed period.

**Earnings per share** follows the same pattern. Basic EPS was negative in FY2013 (₹(0.45)) and FY2014 (₹(0.20)), then turned positive: ₹2.47 in FY2015, ₹1.46 in FY2016, ₹2.43 in FY2017, and ₹1.48 for the six months to September 2017. [^drhp-p177] The dip in FY2016 — PAT fell from ₹149.58 lakh to ₹90.18 lakh, a drop of about 40% before recovering in FY2017.

**Cash flow from operations** is more volatile than the PAT trend suggests. The numbers, in ₹ lakh:

The FY2014 operating cash outflow of ₹349.75 lakh occurred while the company was also reporting a modest PAT loss — meaning working capital was consuming cash beyond the operating loss. The FY2015 cash inflow of ₹802.59 lakh is the strongest in the series, coinciding with the turnaround year. But cash generation has tapered since: FY2017's ₹127.73 lakh is less than one-sixth of FY2015's level, even though PAT was higher in FY2017. This divergence between profit and cash flow suggests that revenue growth may be consuming working capital (receivables or inventory) faster than profits are generating cash.

The DRHP excerpts do not disclose revenue figures, EBITDA, or debt levels. Without revenue, it is not possible to compute margins or assess the company's top-line growth trajectory from the filing data alone.

## Promoters, shareholding and governance

The promoters are Mr. Chandresh S. Parikh, Mr. Atil C. Parikh, Mr. Rajesh C. Parikh, and the corporate entity 20 Microns Limited. [^drhp-p155-156] The promoter shareholding is extraordinarily concentrated: the four promoters together hold 89,48,270 equity shares, representing 99.76% of the pre-issue paid-up equity share capital. [^drhp-p155] The promoter group (excluding the promoters themselves) holds only 21,510 shares, or 0.24%. [^drhp-p162] This means that before the IPO, virtually every share in the company is held by the four promoters.

The authorised share capital is ₹2,000 lakh (20,00,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each), and the issued, subscribed and paid-up capital is ₹897 lakh (89,70,000 equity shares of ₹10 each) as at September 30, 2017. [^drhp-p190] The gap between authorised and issued capital means the company has headroom to issue additional shares without amending its capital clause.

Related party transactions have occurred over the last five financial years and the six months ended September 30, 2017, as per Accounting Standard 18. [^drhp-p166] The DRHP cross-references page 223 for details, which are not included in the provided excerpts. Given that the company sells through its corporate promoter's network, related party transactions are inherent to the business model. The marketing arrangement itself is a related party transaction.

The company, its promoters, group companies and directors are parties to certain legal proceedings. [^drhp-p17] The filing warns that any adverse outcome could materially affect business, results of operations and financial condition. [^drhp-p17]

There have been instances of non-filing or incorrect or delayed filings with the Registrar of Companies. [^drhp-p18] The company has not yet obtained all statutory licenses and registrations required to operate one of its manufacturing facilities: Plot 9-10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat. [^drhp-p19] The filing states that the company cannot assure it will not be penalised for such non-compliance under relevant laws. [^drhp-p19] This is a concrete regulatory risk that goes beyond boilerplate.

## Key risks

Five material risks specific to this company, each grounded in the filing:

**1. No long-term agreements with customers or suppliers.** The company operates entirely on purchase orders. It has not entered into any long-term or definitive agreements with customers to govern volume, pricing or other terms of sales. [^drhp-p26] Similarly, it has no long-term agreements with raw material suppliers. [^drhp-p25] This exposes the company to volume volatility, pricing pressure, and supply disruptions. Any of these could affect operations and profitability without contractual protection.

**2. Negative cash flows from operations in the past.** The company had an operating cash outflow of ₹349.75 lakh in FY2014. [^drhp-p50] While cash generation turned positive in subsequent years, the FY2017 operating cash flow of ₹127.73 lakh was modest relative to the PAT of ₹174.82 lakh. Sustained negative cash flow could impair the company's ability to fund growth or service obligations.

**3. Missing statutory licenses for a manufacturing facility.** The company has not obtained certain statutory and regulatory licenses, registrations and approvals required to operate its manufacturing facility at Plot 9-10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat. [^drhp-p19] The filing acknowledges the risk of penalties for non-compliance under relevant laws, which could disrupt operations at that facility.

**4. Extreme promoter concentration.** Promoters hold 99.76% of pre-issue equity. [^drhp-p155] This creates potential for conflicts of interest and leaves minority shareholders with limited influence over corporate decisions.

**5. Legal proceedings involving the company, promoters and group entities.** The company, its promoters, group companies and directors are parties to certain legal proceedings. [^drhp-p17] An adverse outcome could have a material adverse effect on business, results of operations and financial condition. [^drhp-p17]
