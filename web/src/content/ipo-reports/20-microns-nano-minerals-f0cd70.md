20 Microns Nano Minerals Limited processes and sells specialty chemicals: functional additives, chemically modified minerals, and soft and hard minerals. [^drhp-p112] It wants to raise money in an SME IPO to build a new plant and list its shares on NSE EMERGE, the exchange's platform for smaller companies. [^drhp-p75]

What makes the business unusual is not the products but the route to market: the company sells through the marketing network of its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, under a long-term arrangement. [^drhp-p112] This report reads the company's Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP), the document it filed with SEBI ahead of the issue, and sets out what the filing discloses about the business, the industry, the offer, the financials and the risks. It is built entirely from that filing; where the filing does not disclose something, this report says so rather than guessing.

## What the company does

The core of the business is processing minerals and chemicals. The filing lists four product lines: functional additives (FA), chemically modified minerals (CMM), soft minerals (SM) and hard minerals (HM). [^drhp-p112] The company takes raw mineral inputs and processes them into specialty chemicals: products added to other materials to give them specific properties, rather than sold as commodities. The company cites a diversified product portfolio and diversified user industries among its strengths, meaning it does not depend on a single product or a single end-market. [^drhp-p113]

The company also claims import substitution as a strength. [^drhp-p113] This matters because India's chemicals industry has consistently imported more than it has exported: over the five years from 2012-13 to 2016-17, imports grew at a 6.4% CAGR against 6% for exports. [^drhp-p92] In the filing's telling, a domestic producer that can replace imported specialty chemicals is positioned on the right side of India's trade flows.

The route to market runs through the promoter's network. The company says it derives substantial benefits from 20 Microns Limited, its corporate promoter, and that a long-term arrangement lets it market and sell its products through the promoter's network. [^drhp-p112-113] This is a double-edged feature: it gives the company a ready distribution channel, but it also makes the business dependent on a related party, a point the filing itself acknowledges as a risk, discussed later.

Customer concentration is high. The top 10 customers accounted for around 59% of revenue from operations in FY2015, 57% in FY2016, and 59% in FY2017. [^drhp-p128] That is a concentrated book: roughly three-fifths of the company's revenue comes from ten buyers. The filing also discloses that over the past five years the company exported ₹10.68 crore worth of goods, naming Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Sri Lanka as destinations. [^drhp-p128]

The company lists product innovation and continuous R&D as a competitive strength, alongside a strong management team. [^drhp-p45] These are claims the company makes about itself; the available excerpts of the DRHP do not quantify R&D spend or patent holdings.

## The market and its headwinds

The specialty chemicals segment of India's chemicals industry was worth about ₹1,000 billion in fiscal 2017, roughly 17% of the total chemicals pie, which CRISIL Research estimated at ₹5,800–6,200 billion that year. [^drhp-p92] Specialty chemicals grew at 10–12% CAGR from fiscal 2014 to fiscal 2017, and CRISIL projects 12–14% CAGR. [^drhp-p94]

Within that broad segment, the filing highlights faster-growing downstream niches. Construction chemicals, water chemicals and personal care ingredients are each expected to expand at 15% CAGR; polymer additives at 10%; textile chemicals at 12%; flavours and fragrances at 12%; surfactants at 13%. [^drhp-p94]

The industry is highly fragmented: SMEs hold 70–75% of the market, and only a few Indian players have the scale or capability to compete with global giants on product development and innovation. [^drhp-p94-95] For a small company, fragmentation cuts both ways: it means room to operate without being crushed by a dominant leader, but it also means the field is crowded and price-driven.

The demand drivers the filing cites are the standard Indian consumption story: rising consumption intensity, urbanisation (urban population was about 31% in 2011), and government initiatives including a draft National Chemical Policy aimed at increasing the chemicals sector's share of GDP, plus approval for FDI of up to 100% in the sector. [^drhp-p90-96] The filing also notes that China's stricter environmental regulations since 2015 have constrained its chemical output, a favourable global factor for Indian producers. [^drhp-p96]

The headwinds are named too. REACH, the European Union's Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals regulation, governs exports to European markets. [^drhp-p110] GST came into effect in India from July 1, 2017, replacing multiple indirect taxes with a single one. [^drhp-p111]

## The offer and what the money is for

The stated objects of the issue are three: to fund capital expenditure on a new manufacturing facility at Plot 336, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat; to serve general corporate purposes; and to create a public trading market for the shares by listing on NSE EMERGE, the SME platform of the National Stock Exchange, to enhance visibility and brand. [^drhp-p75]

The lead manager is Vivro Financial Services Private Limited, and the registrar is Link Intime India Private Limited. [^drhp-p54-55]

One gap must be flagged: the available excerpts of the DRHP do not disclose the issue size, the price band, or whether there is an offer-for-sale component (where existing shareholders sell some of their shares, with the proceeds going to them rather than the company). [^drhp-p75] This report does not speculate on those figures. What the filing does make clear is the purpose (a new plant and listing) and the service providers.

The profit story is one of a turnaround. The company lost ₹0.27 crore in FY2013 and ₹0.12 crore in FY2014, then turned profitable in FY2015 with ₹1.50 crore, dipped to ₹0.90 crore in FY2016, and recovered to ₹1.75 crore in FY2017. [^drhp-p49] The first half of FY2018, the six months to September 30, 2017, produced ₹1.33 crore, already three-quarters of the full FY2017 figure. [^drhp-p49] Basic earnings per share track the same arc: negative ₹0.45 and ₹0.20 in FY2013 and FY2014, then positive ₹2.47, ₹1.46, ₹2.43 and ₹1.48 across FY2015, FY2016, FY2017 and H1 FY2018. [^drhp-p177]

The cash flow story is more complicated, and it is the single most striking tension in the restated statements. In FY2017, the company reported a profit of ₹1.75 crore but generated a ₹3.50 crore *outflow* from operations. [^drhp-p49-50] That is a sharp divergence: profit on paper, cash leaving the business. The contrast with the prior year is stark: FY2016 produced an ₹8.03 crore operating cash inflow on a profit of just ₹0.90 crore. [^drhp-p49-50] The pattern across the full six periods: inflows of ₹2.01 crore (FY2013), ₹1.28 crore (FY2014), ₹4.22 crore (FY2015), ₹8.03 crore (FY2016), then the ₹3.50 crore outflow in FY2017, and a return to a ₹1.25 crore inflow in H1 FY2018. [^drhp-p50] The FY2017 outflow is the anomaly, a year when reported profit and operating cash moved in opposite directions. The filing itself warns that the company had negative cash flows in previous financial years. [^drhp-p22]

Total assets tell a story of a business that shrank before it grew. Assets stood at ₹30.19 crore at March 31, 2013, dipped to ₹25.93 crore by FY2015, then climbed steadily to ₹32.74 crore at FY2017 and ₹34.25 crore at September 30, 2017. [^drhp-p175] The company also discloses contingent liabilities (potential obligations that have not yet crystallised) aggregating ₹29.11 lakh as of September 30, 2017. [^drhp-p21]

Two gaps must be flagged. The available excerpts do not include a revenue-from-operations line, so this report cannot state the company's top line or its growth. [^drhp-p49] Nor do they include EBITDA, borrowings, or net worth figures. [^drhp-p175] The profitability trend is best read alongside the cash flow; the FY2017 divergence shows why.

## Promoters, control and governance flags

The promoters are 20 Microns Limited (the corporate promoter) and three individuals: Chandresh S. Parikh, Atil C. Parikh and Rajesh C. Parikh. [^drhp-p155-156]

Control is absolute. Pre-issue, the promoters hold 99.76% of the paid-up equity capital (89,48,270 shares), and the promoter group holds the remaining 0.24% (21,510 shares). [^drhp-p155-162] Every share of the company is held by the promoters and their group before the issue; there is no public float at all. [^drhp-p155-162]

The capital structure is modest. Authorised share capital is ₹20 crore (2 crore equity shares of ₹10 each), while issued, subscribed and paid-up capital is ₹8.97 crore (89.7 lakh shares). [^drhp-p190] The company has room to issue more shares within its authorised capital. [^drhp-p190]

The governance disclosures carry several flags. The company has had related-party transactions in each of the last five financial years and in the six months to September 30, 2017, as per Accounting Standard 18. [^drhp-p166] Given that the company sells through its corporate promoter's network, related-party dealings are structural to the business model, not incidental. [^drhp-p112]

The filing also discloses past instances of non-filing or delayed filings with the Registrar of Companies, which in certain instances were subsequently filed along with additional fees. [^drhp-p18] And it says the company has not yet obtained certain statutory and regulatory licenses, registrations and approvals required to operate its manufacturing facility at Plot 9-10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, and cannot assure it will not be penalised for such non-compliance. [^drhp-p19] Outstanding legal proceedings involve the company, its promoters, group companies and directors, and any adverse outcome could have a material adverse effect on the business. [^drhp-p17]

## Material risks in the filing

The DRHP lists 60 risk factors. [^drhp-p34] Most are boilerplate that would appear in any Indian prospectus. The five below are specific to this company, each tied to a concrete disclosure in the filing.

**Customer concentration without contracts.** Around 59% of revenue comes from the top 10 customers, and the company has not entered into any long-term or definitive agreements with its customers; it relies on purchase orders to govern volume, pricing and other terms. [^drhp-p26-128] A large customer can walk away or squeeze prices without breaching any contract. The same applies to raw material suppliers: no long-term agreements, so the company is exposed to timely availability and price swings. [^drhp-p25]

**Negative operating cash flow.** FY2017 produced a ₹3.50 crore outflow from operations despite a reported profit of ₹1.75 crore. [^drhp-p49-50] The company itself warns that it had negative cash flows in previous financial years. [^drhp-p22] A company that reports profit but burns cash in operations is financing its working capital from somewhere. The filing notes that unsecured loans taken by the company, the promoters, group companies or associates can be recalled by lenders at any time. [^drhp-p24]

**It does not own its premises.** The company does not own the premises where two of its manufacturing facilities are located, Plot 347, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat, and Plot 104/3, Thenkasi Road, Tirunelveli, Tamil Nadu, nor its administrative offices in Bhuj and Udaipur. [^drhp-p20] A landlord who does not renew a lease could halt operations at short notice.

**Missing statutory approvals.** The company has not yet obtained all statutory and regulatory licenses, registrations and approvals needed to operate its manufacturing facility at Plot 9-10, GIDC Waghodia, and cannot assure it will not be penalised for non-compliance. [^drhp-p19]

**Related-party dependence.** The business depends on its corporate promoter's marketing network to sell its products, and the company has a history of related-party transactions. [^drhp-p112-166] The filing itself acknowledges that such transactions, individually or in aggregate, could have an adverse effect on financial condition and results of operations. [^drhp-p24] For a company where 99.76% of shares are held by the promoters, the line between the company's interest and the promoter group's interest is one the board will have to police carefully. [^drhp-p155]
