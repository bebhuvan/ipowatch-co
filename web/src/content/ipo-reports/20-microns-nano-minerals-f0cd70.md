20 Microns Nano Minerals is a B2B processor of minerals into specialty chemicals (functional additives, chemically modified minerals, soft minerals and hard minerals) that sells largely through a long-term marketing arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited.[^drhp-p112-113] The company is going public on NSE EMERGE, the SME platform of the National Stock Exchange, with the stated objects of building a new plant at GIDC Waghodia in Vadodara, funding general corporate purposes, and creating a public market for its shares.[^drhp-p75] Its promoters already own 99.76% of the pre-issue equity.[^drhp-p155] This article is built only from the DRHP; where the filing does not disclose a number, that is stated rather than estimated.

## What the company does

Minerals are the raw material; chemistry is the work. The company buys or processes minerals and sells them as specialised chemical products for industrial buyers. Its product range is described in four buckets: Functional Additives and Chemically Modified Minerals, plus Soft Minerals and Hard Minerals.[^drhp-p112] These are inputs for other manufacturers, a paint additive or a water-treatment chemical, rather than finished goods sold to consumers.

The route to market is unusual. The company has a long-term arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, to market and sell its products through that promoter's network.[^drhp-p112-113] The filing frames this as a strength: the company says it derives "substantial synergies" from the holding company.[^drhp-p113] But the same fact is a reliance risk: the company's sales channel is its promoter's.[^drhp-p112] If that relationship weakens, the company is left without the distribution network it describes.[^drhp-p112]

Customer concentration is high. The top 10 customers accounted for around 59% of revenue from operations in FY2015, 57% in FY2016 and 59% in FY2017.[^drhp-p128] Export markets named in the filing are Nigeria, Saudi Arabia and Sri Lanka.[^drhp-p128]

The physical footprint includes manufacturing facilities at GIDC Waghodia in Gujarat and Tirunelveli in Tamil Nadu, with administrative offices in Bhuj and Udaipur, none of which are owned by the company.[^drhp-p20] The DRHP lists its competitive strengths as a diversified product portfolio, diversified end-user industries, import substitution, holding-company support, continuous R&D, and a strong management team.[^drhp-p45-113]

## Industry and market

Specialty chemicals are formulated for a specific job rather than sold as a generic commodity. The company sits inside India's chemicals industry, which CRISIL Research estimated at ₹5,800–6,200 billion in fiscal 2017, excluding pharmaceuticals and fertilisers.[^drhp-p92] Within that, specialty chemicals excluding agrochemicals and dyestuff account for about 17%, roughly ₹1,000 billion.[^drhp-p92] A separate estimate in the filing puts the segment at ₹1,443 billion, a figure the filing does not reconcile.[^drhp-p94]

The segment is growing. Specialty chemicals grew at 10–12% CAGR from fiscal 2014 to 2017, and CRISIL Research forecasts 12–14% CAGR going forward.[^drhp-p94] The filing names fast-growing sub-segments: construction chemicals, water chemicals and personal care ingredients each expected to grow at 15% CAGR; surfactants at 13%; textile chemicals and flavours-fragrances at 12%; polymer additives at 10%.[^drhp-p94]

The macro backdrop is consumption-led. India's GDP grew 7.1% in fiscal 2017, private consumption is 58% of GDP, and end-use demand is expected to rise with a middle class that could reach 148 million households by 2030.[^drhp-p87-95] The government has a draft National Chemical Policy aimed at increasing the chemicals sector's share of GDP, and 100% FDI is allowed in the sector.[^drhp-p95-96]

Imports have grown faster than exports in Indian chemicals: 6.4% CAGR versus 6% for exports from fiscal 2013 to 2017, which the filing links to its stated import-substitution strength.[^drhp-p92-113] Competition is fragmented: SMEs hold 70–75% of the market, and only a few Indian players have the scale or capability to compete with global giants on product development and innovation.[^drhp-p94-95]

Regulation shapes the industry too. REACH compliance matters for exports to Europe, GST took effect from July 1 2017, and four Petroleum, Chemicals and Petrochemicals Investment Regions (PCPIRs) have been approved in Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Odisha, with about ₹9 trillion in proposed investment.[^drhp-p110-111]

## The offer and its objects

The stated primary object is funding capital expenditure for a new manufacturing facility at Plot 336, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat.[^drhp-p75] The other stated objects are general corporate purposes and creating a public trading market for the equity shares by listing on NSE EMERGE to enhance visibility and brand.[^drhp-p75]

The lead manager is Vivro Financial Services Private Limited, and the registrar is Link Intime India Private Limited.[^drhp-p54-55]

The offer size, the fresh-issue versus offer-for-sale split, any selling shareholder and the price band are not present in the extracted pages; they are treated here as not disclosed.

## Financial picture

The restated financial statements are prior-year accounts presented again under uniform accounting policies for the IPO; they can differ from what the company first published. The restated financials cover six months ended September 30 2017 and fiscal years ended March 2017, 2016, 2015, 2014 and 2013.[^drhp-p49] Amounts in the DRHP are in ₹ lakh; divide by 100 to get ₹ crore.

Profit after tax turned positive in fiscal 2015. The company went from a ₹0.27 crore loss in FY13 and a ₹0.12 crore loss in FY14 to ₹1.50 crore in FY15, ₹0.90 crore in FY16, ₹1.75 crore in FY17 and ₹1.33 crore in H1 FY18.[^drhp-p49] The turnaround is real but the scale is modest: the company has never earned more than ₹1.75 crore in a full year.[^drhp-p49]

Operating cash flow has been uneven: +₹2.01 crore in FY13, +₹1.28 crore in FY14, +₹4.22 crore in FY15, +₹8.03 crore in FY16, then −₹3.50 crore in FY17, and +₹1.25 crore in H1 FY18.[^drhp-p50] The swing from +₹8.03 crore to −₹3.50 crore in a single year is the kind of volatility that matters for a company this size. The filing itself lists negative cash flows in previous financial years as an internal risk factor.[^drhp-p22]

Total assets stood at ₹34.25 crore as of September 30 2017, up from ₹30.19 crore in FY13, with a dip in FY14–FY15.[^drhp-p175] Basic earnings per share: ₹2.47 in FY15, ₹1.46 in FY16, ₹2.43 in FY17 and ₹1.48 in H1 FY18; negative in FY13 and FY14.[^drhp-p177]

Revenue from operations by period and EBITDA are not in the extracted statements; debt levels are not given either.

## Ownership and governance

The promoters are Mr Chandresh S. Parikh, Mr Atil C. Parikh, Mr Rajesh C. Parikh, and corporate promoter 20 Microns Limited.[^drhp-p155-156] Promoters hold 89,48,270 equity shares, 99.76% of the pre-issue paid-up capital, and promoter group members outside the promoters hold 21,510 shares, or 0.24%.[^drhp-p155-162]

As at September 30 2017, authorised share capital was ₹2,000 lakh and issued, subscribed and paid-up capital was ₹897.00 lakh, in shares of ₹10 each.[^drhp-p190]

Related-party transactions are disclosed for the last five financial years and the six months ended September 30 2017,[^drhp-p166] and the company says it has entered into related-party transactions in the past and may continue to do so.[^drhp-p24] The overlaps are structural: the corporate promoter both owns 99.76% of the equity and is the marketing channel, so related-party exposure is intertwined with the business model itself.[^drhp-p112-155]

Governance flags disclosed in the filing: legal proceedings involving the company, promoters, group company and directors; premises for plants and offices not owned; past delays or incorrect filings with the RoC; a corporate logo not yet registered; and certain statutory licences for one GIDC Waghodia facility not yet obtained.[^drhp-p17-20]

## The risks that matter

**Order-driven revenue.** The company has no long-term or definitive agreements with customers or raw-material suppliers; volume, price and terms rest on purchase orders, so revenue can move with short-term orders.[^drhp-p25-26]

**The route to market belongs to the promoter.** The company relies on a long-term arrangement under which 20 Microns Limited markets and sells its products; a weakening of that relationship would leave the company without the distribution network the filing describes.[^drhp-p112-113]

**Licensing and compliance gaps.** The filing admits certain statutory licences and approvals needed to operate the manufacturing facility at Plot 9–10, GIDC Waghodia, have not yet been obtained, and there have been past instances of delayed or incorrect filings with the RoC.[^drhp-p18-19]

**Cash flow is volatile.** Operating cash flow swung from +₹8.03 crore in FY16 to −₹3.50 crore in FY17, and the DRHP flags negative cash flows in prior financial years.[^drhp-p22-50]

**Financial and legal overhangs.** Contingent liabilities aggregated ₹29.11 lakh as at September 30 2017, unsecured loans can be recalled by lenders at any time, the company faces product-liability exposure, and it is party to certain legal proceedings.[^drhp-p17-27]
