This is a company that processes minerals into specialty chemicals and sells them almost entirely through its parent's distribution network. 20 Microns Nano Minerals Limited grinds and modifies minerals — calcium carbonate, talc, barite, and others — into fine powders that manufacturers add to plastics, paints, adhesives, and construction materials. The company relies on its parent's distribution network to market and sell its products, as per a long-term arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited.[^drhp-p112] The arrangement is the spine of the business model: the parent handles the customer relationships, and the subsidiary focuses on processing.

The company is now seeking to list on the SME platform of NSE (NSE EMERGE) through a fresh issue of equity shares.[^drhp-p75] The proceeds are earmarked for capital expenditure on a new manufacturing facility at Plot 336, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat, and for general corporate purposes.[^drhp-p75] The exact number of shares and the price band are not disclosed in the filing. The lead manager to the issue is Vivro Financial Services Private Limited, and the registrar is Link Intime India Private Limited.[^drhp-p54-55]

## What the company does

The company sells four categories of processed minerals. Functional Additives (FA) and Chemically Modified Minerals (CMM) are the primary product lines; Soft Minerals (SM) and Hard Minerals (HM) are secondary.[^drhp-p112] The company lists six competitive strengths in its filing: a diversified product portfolio, diversified user industries, import substitution, the support of its holding company, product innovation and continuous R&D, and a strong management team.[^drhp-p45-113]

The customer base is concentrated. In each of the three financial years FY2015, FY2016, and FY2017, the top 10 customers accounted for around 59%, 57%, and 59% of revenue from operations, respectively.[^drhp-p128] That level of concentration means the loss of a single large customer could materially affect revenue. The company has not entered into any long-term or definitive agreements with its customers; all sales are governed by individual purchase orders.[^drhp-p26] The same is true on the supply side: there are no long-term contracts with raw material suppliers.[^drhp-p25]

Export activity is narrow. Over the past five years, the company achieved total exports of ₹10.68 crore, shipped to just three countries: Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, and Sri Lanka.[^drhp-p128] The company acknowledges it is subject to product liability claims with respect to quality and defects in India as well as in those export markets.[^drhp-p27]

## Industry and market

The Indian specialty chemicals segment — excluding agrochemicals and dyestuff — was estimated at roughly ₹1,000 billion in FY2017, accounting for about 17% of the broader Indian chemicals industry (which CRISIL Research estimated at ₹5,800–6,200 billion in FY2017, excluding pharmaceuticals and fertilisers).[^drhp-p92] The segment grew at 10–12% CAGR from FY2014 to FY2017, and CRISIL Research projects it will grow at 12–14% CAGR going forward.[^drhp-p94]

Within specialty chemicals, several sub-segments are expected to grow faster than the overall market. Construction chemicals and personal care ingredients are each projected to grow at 15% CAGR over the next three years, while water chemicals is projected to grow at 15% CAGR over the next five years. Surfactants are forecast at 13% CAGR over the next five years, textile chemicals at 12% CAGR, and polymer additives at 10% CAGR over the next three years.[^drhp-p94]

The industry is highly fragmented, with small and medium enterprises holding 70–75% market share. Only a few Indian players have the scale or capability to compete with global giants on product development and innovation.[^drhp-p94-95] The company positions itself as an import-substitution play: India's chemicals imports have grown at 6.4% CAGR over the five years to FY2017, outpacing exports at 6% CAGR, and the company cites import substitution as one of its competitive strengths.[^drhp-p92-113]

The regulatory environment is evolving. The government has released a Draft National Chemical Policy aimed at increasing the chemicals sector's share of GDP, and has approved 100% foreign direct investment in the sector.[^drhp-p95-96] Four Petroleum, Chemicals and Petrochemicals Investment Regions (PCPIRs) have been approved in Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Odisha, with total proposed investments of about ₹9 trillion.[^drhp-p111] The Goods and Services Tax came into effect from July 1, 2017, replacing multiple indirect taxes with a single tax.[^drhp-p111] For exports to Europe, the company must comply with the Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH) regulation.[^drhp-p110]

## Financial health

The company's financial story over the five full financial years from FY2013 to FY2017 is one of a turnaround from losses to consistent profitability, followed by a sharp reversal in operating cash flow in the most recent year.

The company was loss-making in FY2013 and FY2014, with net losses of ₹0.27 crore and ₹0.12 crore respectively. It turned profitable in FY2015, posting a net profit of ₹1.50 crore, and has remained profitable since. FY2017 was the best year for the bottom line, with a net profit of ₹1.75 crore. The six months to September 2017 already showed a profit of ₹1.33 crore, suggesting FY2018 could exceed the prior year's performance.[^drhp-p49]

The cash flow story is more volatile. Net cash from operations was positive and growing through FY2015 and FY2016 — ₹4.22 crore and ₹8.03 crore respectively — but swung sharply negative in FY2017 to ₹(3.50) crore.[^drhp-p50] The filing does not explicitly explain the cause of this swing, but it is a material risk: a company that cannot generate positive operating cash flow must rely on financing or asset sales to fund its operations, and its unsecured loans are recallable at any time.[^drhp-p24-50] In H1FY2018, operating cash flow recovered to ₹1.25 crore — positive but still below the levels seen in FY2015 and FY2016.[^drhp-p50]

Total assets stood at ₹34.25 crore as of September 30, 2017, up from ₹30.19 crore in FY2013.[^drhp-p175] The company has unsecured loans that can be recalled by lenders at any time, and its loan agreements contain restrictive covenants that could influence its ability to expand.[^drhp-p24]

Basic earnings per share followed the profit trajectory: from a loss of ₹0.45 per share in FY2013 to a profit of ₹2.47 in FY2015, dipping to ₹1.46 in FY2016, recovering to ₹2.43 in FY2017, and ₹1.48 in the six months to September 2017.[^drhp-p177]

The DRHP does not disclose a peer comparison table. It does not provide P/E multiples, earnings per share, net asset value, or return on net worth for any named competitor. The only industry context it offers is the description of the specialty chemicals market as highly fragmented, with SMEs holding 70–75% market share and only a few Indian players competing with global giants.[^drhp-p94-95]

Without a price band or peer multiples, a valuation analysis cannot be performed from the DRHP alone. ## Ownership and governance

The company is tightly held. The four promoters — 20 Microns Limited (the corporate promoter), Mr. Chandresh S. Parikh, Mr. Atil C. Parikh, and Mr. Rajesh C. Parikh — collectively hold 89,48,270 equity shares, representing 99.76% of the pre-issue paid-up equity share capital.[^drhp-p155-156] The promoter group (excluding the promoters themselves) holds an additional 21,510 shares, or 0.24% of pre-issue capital.[^drhp-p162] Public holding before the issue is nil.[^drhp-p155-162]

The authorised share capital is ₹2,000 lakh, while the issued, subscribed, and paid-up capital as of September 30, 2017, was ₹897 lakh (89,70,000 equity shares of ₹10 each).[^drhp-p190] The fresh issue will increase the paid-up capital, but the exact number of shares to be issued is not disclosed.

Related-party transactions have occurred in each of the last five financial years and the six months ended September 30, 2017, as detailed in the financial statements under Accounting Standard 18.[^drhp-p166] The company acknowledges that such transactions may continue in the future and that there can be no assurance they will not have an adverse effect on financial condition and results of operations.[^drhp-p24]

There are compliance flags. The company has had instances of non-filing, incorrect filings, or delays in filing statutory forms with the Registrar of Companies, though these were subsequently filed along with payment of additional fees.[^drhp-p18] More significantly, the company has not yet obtained certain statutory and regulatory licences, registrations, and approvals required to operate its manufacturing facility at Plot 9–10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat, and cannot assure that it will not be penalised for such non-compliance.[^drhp-p19]

The company does not own the premises for two of its manufacturing facilities (Plot 347, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat, and Plot 104/3, Thenkasi Road, Pathur Village, Alangulam, Tirunelveli, Tamil Nadu) nor for its administrative offices in Bhuj and Udaipur.[^drhp-p20] It has also not yet obtained registration for its corporate logo.[^drhp-p19]

## Top material risks

**1. No long-term customer or supplier agreements.** The company operates entirely on purchase orders from customers and has no long-term contracts with raw material suppliers. This exposes it to volume and price volatility on both the revenue and cost sides. A customer can stop ordering with no notice; a supplier can raise prices or stop supply.[^drhp-p25-26]

**2. Negative operating cash flow in FY2017.** After generating ₹8.03 crore in operating cash flow in FY2016, the company swung to a negative ₹3.50 crore in FY2017. The filing does not explain the cause. If this pattern recurs, the company may need to rely on debt or equity financing to fund operations, and its unsecured loans are recallable at any time.[^drhp-p24-50]

**3. Pending statutory licences for a manufacturing facility.** The company has not obtained required licences and registrations for its facility at Plot 9–10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat. It faces the risk of penalties or even shutdown of that facility under relevant laws.[^drhp-p19]

**4. Product liability exposure in domestic and export markets.** The company sells in India and in three export markets (Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, and Sri Lanka)[^drhp-p128] and is subject to product liability claims for defects. A significant claim could harm reputation and finances.[^drhp-p27]

**5. Unsecured loans recallable at any time.** Loans taken by the company and its promoters can be recalled by lenders at any time, creating refinancing risk. Loan agreements also contain restrictive covenants that could limit the company's ability to expand.[^drhp-p24]
