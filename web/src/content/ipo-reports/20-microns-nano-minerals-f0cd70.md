20 Microns Nano Minerals Limited processes and sells specialty chemicals: minerals that have been ground, treated, or chemically modified to serve as additives in paints, plastics, cosmetics, and construction materials. [^drhp-p112] The company is raising capital through an IPO on NSE EMERGE, the SME platform of the National Stock Exchange, to fund a new manufacturing facility in Gujarat and for general corporate purposes. This report is built entirely from the company's Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP).

## What the company does

The company's business is straightforward: it takes raw minerals and turns them into functional ingredients that other manufacturers add to their products. These are not finished goods sold on a shelf; they are the invisible components that give paint its opacity, plastic its strength, or cosmetics their texture.

The product portfolio divides into four categories. Functional Additives (FA) and Chemically Modified Minerals (CMM) form the core of the business, supplemented by Soft Minerals (SM) and Hard Minerals (HM). [^drhp-p112] The company lists a diversified product portfolio and diversified user industries among its competitive strengths. [^drhp-p113]

What makes the business model unusual is its dependence on a single marketing channel. The company has a long-term arrangement with its corporate promoter, 20 Microns Limited, to market and sell its products through that company's network. [^drhp-p112] This is presented as a strength: the company derives "substantial synergies" from the promoter's established distribution reach. [^drhp-p113] But it is also a concentration risk: the company does not own its own sales channel and relies on a related party to reach its customers.

Customer concentration is high. In FY2015, FY2016, and FY2017, the top 10 customers accounted for approximately 59%, 57%, and 59% of revenue from operations, respectively. [^drhp-p128] Losing even one of these customers would have a material impact on revenue.

<aside class="pull-quote">In FY2015, FY2016, and FY2017, the top 10 customers accounted for approximately 59%, 57%, and 59% of revenue from operations, respectively.</aside>

The company's export business is small. Over the past five years, it achieved total exports of ₹10.68 crore, shipping to Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, and Sri Lanka. [^drhp-p128] The export numbers are small relative to the overall business, but they show that the company can compete outside India.

## Industry and market

India's specialty chemicals industry was worth about ₹1,00,000 crore in FY2017, and it is growing. CRISIL Research estimated the specialty chemicals segment at approximately ₹1,00,000 crore (₹1,000 billion) in FY2017, representing about 17% of the broader Indian chemicals industry, which itself was valued at ₹5,80,000–6,20,000 crore. [^drhp-p92] The segment grew at 10–12% CAGR from FY2014 to FY2017, and CRISIL forecasts acceleration to 12–14% CAGR over the next few years. [^drhp-p94]

Within specialty chemicals, certain sub-segments are expected to grow even faster. Construction chemicals and personal care ingredients are each forecast to grow at 15% CAGR over the next three years. [^drhp-p94] These are precisely the kinds of end-markets that 20 Microns Nano Minerals serves, suggesting tailwinds for demand.

The industry structure matters for a small player. The market is highly fragmented, with small and medium enterprises holding 70–75% of the share. [^drhp-p94] Only a few Indian companies have the scale or capability to compete with global giants on product development and innovation. [^drhp-p95] This fragmentation means that a focused SME can carve out a defensible niche without facing direct competition from multinationals in every product category.

Several macro factors support the industry's growth. India's GDP grew 7.1% in FY2017, and private consumption, the largest contributor to GDP at 58%, continues to rise. [^drhp-p87-88] The government has released a Draft National Chemical Policy aimed at increasing the chemicals sector's share of GDP, and has allowed 100% foreign direct investment in the sector. [^drhp-p95-96]

One trend: India's chemicals industry has consistently imported more than it has exported over the five years from FY2013 to FY2017, with imports growing at 6.4% CAGR versus exports at 6% CAGR. [^drhp-p92] This trade deficit represents an opportunity for import substitution, one of the company's listed competitive strengths. [^drhp-p113]

## The offer

The DRHP does not disclose the number of shares being offered, the price band, or whether the issue includes a fresh issue component alongside an offer for sale. These details will be set in the final prospectus. What is clear are the objects of the issue: the purposes for which the company is raising capital.

The primary object is to fund capital expenditure for setting up a new manufacturing facility at Plot 336, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat. [^drhp-p75] The secondary objects are general corporate purposes and creating a public trading market for the company's equity shares by listing them on the SME Platform of NSE (NSE EMERGE) to increase visibility and brand. [^drhp-p75]

The lead manager to the issue is Vivro Financial Services Private Limited, and the registrar is Link Intime India Private Limited. [^drhp-p54-55]

*Source: PAT and EPS from DRHP pages 49 and 177; cash flow from DRHP page 50. [^drhp-p49-177]*

The company was loss-making in FY2013 and FY2014, with losses of ₹0.27 crore and ₹0.12 crore respectively. It turned profitable in FY2015 with a PAT of ₹1.50 crore, a swing of ₹1.62 crore from the prior year. [^drhp-p49] Profitability has been sustained since, with FY2017 recording the highest full-year profit at ₹1.75 crore. The six-month period ended September 2017 already shows ₹1.33 crore in profit, suggesting the company was on track for another strong year.

Earnings per share followed the same pattern. Basic EPS was negative in FY2013 (₹(0.45)) and FY2014 (₹(0.20)), turned positive at ₹2.47 in FY2015, and has remained positive since, at ₹2.43 for FY2017 and ₹1.48 for the first half of FY2018. [^drhp-p177]

Cash flow from operations was uneven. In FY2013, the company generated ₹2.01 crore in operating cash, but it turned negative in FY2017 with an outflow of ₹3.50 crore. [^drhp-p50] The strongest operating cash flow occurred in FY2016 at ₹8.03 crore, far exceeding the PAT of ₹0.90 crore for that year, suggesting that earnings were supported by aggressive working capital management at that time. In FY2015, operating cash flow was ₹4.22 crore versus PAT of ₹1.50 crore. For the first half of FY2018, operating cash flow was ₹1.25 crore. [^drhp-p50]

The company's total assets stood at ₹34.25 crore as of September 30, 2017, up from ₹32.74 crore at March 31, 2017. [^drhp-p175] The issued, subscribed, and paid-up share capital is ₹8.97 crore, representing 89,70,000 equity shares of ₹10 each. [^drhp-p190]

One gap in the available data: revenue from operations is not disclosed in the extracted financial statements. Without revenue figures, it is impossible to calculate profit margins or revenue growth rates from the filing alone. Investors will need to consult the full DRHP for this information.

## Governance and shareholding

The company's shareholding structure is extremely concentrated. The promoters, 20 Microns Limited (the corporate promoter) and three individuals, Chandresh S. Parikh, Atil C. Parikh, and Rajesh C. Parikh, collectively hold 89,48,270 equity shares, representing 99.76% of the pre-issue paid-up equity share capital. [^drhp-p155-156] The promoter group (excluding the promoters themselves) holds the remaining 21,510 shares, or 0.24%. [^drhp-p162]

This means that before the IPO, the public holds zero shares. The entire equity is in the hands of the promoter family and the corporate promoter. Post-IPO, the public will own whatever percentage the issue size dictates, but the starting point is a company that has been entirely privately held.

Related party transactions are a feature of the company's history. The DRHP discloses that such transactions have occurred during the last five financial years and the six-month period ended September 30, 2017, as detailed in the financial statements under Accounting Standard 18. [^drhp-p166] Given the company's dependence on its corporate promoter for marketing and distribution, related party transactions are not merely incidental; they are central to the business model.

The company, its promoters, group companies, and directors are parties to certain legal proceedings. The DRHP warns that any adverse decision in these proceedings could have a material adverse effect on the business, results of operations, and financial condition. [^drhp-p17]

There have also been compliance issues. The company has missed, incorrectly filed, or delayed statutory forms with the Registrar of Companies (RoC). In certain cases, these have subsequently been filed along with the payment of additional fees. [^drhp-p18] The company has not yet obtained certain statutory and regulatory licenses, registrations, and approvals required to operate its manufacturing facility at Plot 9-10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat. [^drhp-p19] This is the same industrial area where the new facility funded by the IPO proceeds will be located, suggesting that regulatory compliance at this location is an ongoing issue.

## Material risks

Every IPO prospectus contains a risk factors section that runs to dozens of pages. Most are boilerplate. The risks below are specific to this company and grounded in disclosed facts.

**No long-term agreements with customers or suppliers.** The company does not have long-term or definitive agreements with its customers. Instead, it relies on purchase orders to govern the volume, pricing, and other terms of sales. [^drhp-p26] Similarly, it has no long-term agreements with raw material suppliers, exposing it to volatility in availability and pricing. [^drhp-p25] For a company that processes minerals, a commodity input subject to price fluctuations, this lack of contractual certainty is material.

**Missing statutory licenses for a key manufacturing facility.** The company has not obtained certain statutory and regulatory licenses required to operate its manufacturing facility at Plot 9-10, GIDC Waghodia, Vadodara, Gujarat. [^drhp-p19] The DRHP candidly states that the company cannot assure it will not be penalized for this non-compliance. This is not a theoretical risk: the facility is already in operation without the required approvals, exposing the company to potential fines, shutdown orders, or legal action.

**Negative cash flow.** While the company has been profitable since FY2015, its operating cash flow was negative ₹3.50 crore in FY2017. [^drhp-p50] Sustained negative cash flows could hamper the company's ability to fund its capital expenditure plans, including the new facility that is the primary object of this IPO, without additional debt or equity.

**Contingent liabilities.** As of September 30, 2017, the company had contingent liabilities aggregating to ₹29.11 lakh (₹0.29 crore). [^drhp-p21] While this amount is small relative to the company's asset base of ₹34.25 crore, contingent liabilities represent potential future outflows that are not reflected in the profit and loss statement. If these materialize, they would directly reduce profitability.
