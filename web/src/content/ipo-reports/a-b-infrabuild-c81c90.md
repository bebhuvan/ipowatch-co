A B Infrabuild is a Mumbai-based infrastructure company. [^drhp-p63-67] It builds railway platforms, bridges and roads, [^drhp-p71] mostly for government clients, [^drhp-p12] and it runs a ready-mix concrete plant [^drhp-p63], the facility that produces the wet concrete delivered to construction sites in mixer trucks. The company has filed a draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) to raise money. [^drhp-p29] The document describes a business with a concentrated customer base, heavy working-capital needs, and a financial record that shows a shrinking top line alongside a sharp recent jump in profit.

The offer is 44,28,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, at an issue price that has not yet been set. [^drhp-p29] The company says the money will go toward working capital and general corporate purposes. [^drhp-p48] The largest customer is Western Railway, a client since 2006 [^drhp-p67] that accounts for 52% of orders on hand; [^drhp-p65] the largest customers as a group made up 57% of revenue in FY2018. [^drhp-p67] The financial story is a falling top line with a recent profit jump: revenue fell from ₹90.21 crore in FY2015 to ₹60.53 crore in FY2018, while profit after tax jumped to ₹3.15 crore in FY2018 from ₹0.16 crore in FY2017. [^drhp-p27]

## What the company does

A B Infrabuild operates in two segments: infrastructure construction and a ready-mix concrete (RMC) plant [^drhp-p63] at Kashimira in Thane district, Maharashtra. [^drhp-p67] The infrastructure side is the core of the business. Its product lines are railways (designing and constructing platforms, station buildings, gauge conversion and laying railway tracks), bridges of several types (beam, cantilever, arch, suspension, cable-stayed and truss), and roads, both bituminous and concrete. [^drhp-p71]

The way this company wins work is through tenders, and the process is worth understanding because it shapes everything about the business. A client floats a tender for a specific contract. A pre-bid meeting is held to resolve queries. Contractors bid, taking into account their eligibility for that particular contract. The job is awarded to the lowest quote, and a Letter of Acceptance is issued to the lowest bidder. The contractor then deposits a performance guarantee (a security that ensures the work will be completed), and only after that is the site handed over for execution. [^drhp-p72] This is a business where the customer sets the terms, the price is competed down to the lowest bid, and the contractor carries the cost of the guarantee before any work begins.

The client list is dominated by government and public-sector entities: Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai, Western Railway, Central Railway, Mumbai Railway Vikas Corporation, Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority, and Dedicated Freight Corridor Corporation of India, plus private clients including Sky Way Infrastructure Private Limited, Dev Engineers, GCC Hotel Private Ltd and Krypton Corp. [^drhp-p63] Concentration is a defining feature. Western Railway alone contributes 52% of orders on hand [^drhp-p65] and has been a client since 2006. [^drhp-p67] Revenue from the largest customers for the year ended March 31, 2018 amounted to 57% of total revenue. [^drhp-p67]

The filing's claimed strengths are an experienced team with more than 20 years in the infrastructure industry, the box-pushing technique used in executing projects, a modern RMC facility, and a "strong order book". [^drhp-p67] The company describes itself as a major player in developing infrastructure in Mumbai, slowly consolidating its position in Maharashtra. [^drhp-p67] The filing's own SWOT analysis (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats) lists the weaknesses a reader should weigh: the slow pace of government projects, and the risk that a working-capital crunch may affect profitability. [^drhp-p76]

## The market: Indian infrastructure construction

The filing's industry section is the context the company wants investors to see. India's infrastructure sector includes power, bridges, dams, roads and urban infrastructure development. [^drhp-p58] The construction market is highly fragmented and working-capital intensive, contributing roughly 8% to India's GDP at constant prices. [^drhp-p58] The company cites growth evidence: the index of eight core industries grew 4.2% in 2017-18; the logistics sector was expected to grow at a 10.5% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) from US$160 billion in 2017 to US$215 billion by 2020; and India's GDP growth was expected to remain above 7% in 2017. ## The issue and what the money is for

The offer comprises 44,28,000 equity shares of ₹10 each, at an issue price to be determined. [^drhp-p29] The excerpts do not reproduce an explicit "Fresh Issue" label and name no offer-for-sale seller. A fresh issue means new shares sold by the company; an offer for sale means existing shareholders selling into the issue. The disclosed use of proceeds points to the money going to the company itself. The lead manager to the issue is Mark Corporate Advisors Private Limited and the registrar is Bigshare Services Private Limited. [^drhp-p31]

The proceeds have two disclosed uses. The first is ₹1,130 lakhs (₹11.30 crore) for working capital requirements. [^drhp-p48] The second is general corporate purposes, the amount of which is not disclosed in the excerpts. [^drhp-p48] Two process flags appear in the filing. The company has not appointed any independent agency to appraise the proposed project, and no monitoring agency has been appointed; deployment of funds is at the discretion of management and the board, monitored by the audit committee. [^drhp-p18]

## Five years of financials: a shrinking top line, a jump in profit

The restated financial statements in the DRHP run from FY2014 to FY2018, and the clearest trend is falling revenue. Revenue from operations was ₹86.61 crore in FY2014, ₹90.21 crore in FY2015, ₹74.53 crore in FY2016, ₹73.44 crore in FY2017 and ₹60.53 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p27] That is a decline of roughly a third from the FY2015 peak to the FY2018 figure. The company's own summary notes the decrease from ₹90.21 crore in FY2015 to ₹60.53 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p27]

Profit after tax, after adjustments, tells a different story. It was ₹0.96 crore in FY2014, ₹0.78 crore in FY2015, ₹0.33 crore in FY2016, ₹0.16 crore in FY2017 and then ₹3.15 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p27] The jump from ₹0.16 crore to ₹3.15 crore is nearly twenty-fold in a single year, on a revenue line that fell by 18% in the same period. [^drhp-p27] The company's own summary flags this: net profit after adjustments increased sharply from ₹15.86 lakhs in FY2017 to ₹314.66 lakhs in FY2018. [^drhp-p27] The filing's nearest equivalent to EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) is profit before interest, depreciation and tax: ₹7.84 crore in FY2014, ₹9.46 crore in FY2015, ₹7.83 crore in FY2016, ₹6.76 crore in FY2017 and ₹9.29 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p111]

The balance sheet shows where the money sits. Total assets moved from ₹70.62 crore in FY2014 to ₹82.32 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p26] The debt structure is the working-capital story in miniature. Long-term borrowings fell from ₹5.87 crore in FY2014 to ₹1.33 crore in FY2018, while short-term borrowings rose from ₹30.82 crore to ₹35.37 crore over the same period. [^drhp-p26] This is a company financing its operations with short-term money, the kind that must be rolled over constantly and is sensitive to any squeeze in credit availability.

The cash flow picture is the risk the filing itself flags. The company says it had negative cash flow in recent financial years, and that sustained negative cash flow could adversely impact its business, financial condition and results of operations. [^drhp-p16] The filing also discloses ₹405.02 lakhs (₹4.05 crore) of unsecured loans repayable on demand as at March 31, 2018; any demand from lenders for repayment could affect cash flow and financial condition. [^drhp-p16] The read-across is straightforward: this is a small revenue base carrying heavy short-term debt, and the FY2018 profit jump is the number the issue will be priced against.

## Ownership, management and governance flags

The promoter is Amit Bholanath Mishra, [^drhp-p100] who is also the Managing Director. [^drhp-p93] He holds 74,95,476 equity shares, constituting 90.949% of the pre-issue paid-up equity share capital. [^drhp-p100] Three other directors, Bharat Kumar Parmar, Shree Prakash Singh and Mukesh Pandey, each hold 300 equity shares, and are listed as whole-time directors. [^drhp-p93] Hatim Sakerwala separately holds 9% of the company's equity. [^drhp-p98]

The key management personnel are young. The Chief Financial Officer, Anchal Pachori, is 28 years old and a Chartered Accountant; the Company Secretary and Compliance Officer, Mohit Soni, is 26 and an Associate Member of the Institute of Company Secretaries of India. [^drhp-p97] Disclosed transactions include a loan of ₹68.4 lakhs, [^drhp-p129] director remuneration of ₹57.7 lakhs, and payables of ₹324.3 lakhs. [^drhp-p130]

The governance flags in the filing are worth listing. No independent agency has appraised the proposed project, and no monitoring agency has been appointed. [^drhp-p18] Two promoter-promoted companies, Al-Noor Chickens Private Limited and A B Magma Automobiles Private Limited, were struck off by the Registrar of Companies in March 2016 for non-operation of business. [^drhp-p18] Equity shares were issued in the last 12 months at a price that may be lower than the issue price. [^drhp-p16] The company has entered into related-party transactions in the past and may continue to do so. [^drhp-p15]

On one count, the record is clean. None of the directors is on the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) list of wilful defaulters, and no criminal cases have been filed or investigations undertaken against any director. [^drhp-p90] The promoter has confirmed he has not been declared a wilful defaulter by the RBI or any other governmental authority. [^drhp-p100]

## Material risks: five things to weigh

**Customer concentration.** Indian Railways accounted for 57% of revenue in FY2018 [^drhp-p12] and Western Railway accounted for 52% of orders on hand. [^drhp-p65]

**Tender dependence.** Revenue depends on winning government and public-sector undertaking (PSU) bids. If bids fail, or contracts are adjusted, cancelled or suspended by clients, the order book is not necessarily indicative of future revenue or earnings. [^drhp-p12-14]

**Working-capital strain.** The company had negative cash flow in recent years, [^drhp-p16] short-term borrowings of ₹35.37 crore at the end of FY2018, [^drhp-p26] and ₹405.02 lakhs of unsecured loans repayable on demand. [^drhp-p16] Delayed recovery of receivables would affect financial condition. [^drhp-p16]

**Geographic concentration.** Most revenue comes from operations in and around Mumbai. Any adverse development in the region could hurt revenue and results of operations. [^drhp-p13]

**Governance and process risk.** No independent agency has appraised the proposed project and no monitoring agency has been appointed; only audit-committee oversight applies. [^drhp-p18] The promoter's two earlier companies were struck off in 2016. [^drhp-p18] The company, promoter and directors are involved in litigation pending at various stages. [^drhp-p11]
