A B Infrabuild: Inside the DRHP of an Infrastructure Builder

**What the filing says about A B Infrabuild's business, finances, risks and valuation — sourced to the DRHP, page by page.**

A B Infrabuild Limited builds railway platforms, bridges, and roads for the government, and it also runs a ready-mix concrete plant in Thane.[^drhp-p63-71] The company is bringing a fresh issue of 44,28,000 equity shares of ₹10 each.[^drhp-p29] The lead manager is Mark Corporate Advisors Private Limited, and the registrar is Bigshare Services Private Limited.[^drhp-p31]

The company's revenues depend heavily on a single client: Indian Railways, which contributed 57% of revenue in Fiscal 2018. Western Railway, a major client, accounts for 52% of the orders on hand.[^drhp-p12-65] That concentration defines this business, and it runs through every section of the filing.

## What the company does

A B Infrabuild operates in two segments: infrastructure creation — civil construction of railway and road projects — and operating a ready-mix concrete (RMC) plant.[^drhp-p63] The RMC plant, at Kashimira, Thane, supplies concrete for the company's own projects.[^drhp-p67]

The company designs and constructs railway platforms, station buildings, gauge conversions, lays railway tracks, and builds various types of bridges — beam, cantilever, arch, suspension, cable-stayed, and truss. It also constructs bituminous and concrete roads.[^drhp-p71]

Contracts are won through a competitive tender process. The client floats a tender, the company bids, and the work is awarded to the lowest bidder. The company then deposits a performance guarantee before execution begins.[^drhp-p72] This is a standard model for government infrastructure contracts, but it means the company has no control over pricing — it wins by being the cheapest, and margins are determined by how efficiently it executes at that price.

The company's major clients are almost entirely government and public-sector entities: the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai, Western Railway, Central Railway, Mumbai Railway Vikas Corporation Ltd, Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority, and the Dedicated Freight Corridor Corporation of India Ltd. A few private entities — Sky Way Infrastructure Private Limited, Dev Engineers, GCC Hotel Private Ltd, and Krypton Corp — round out the list.[^drhp-p63]

Western Railway has been a client since 2006, and the company points to this long-standing relationship as a competitive strength.[^drhp-p67] Other claimed strengths include quality, transparency in dealings with stakeholders, modern techniques like box pushing (a method for constructing underpasses without disrupting traffic), an experienced management team with over 20 years of industry experience, and the facility at Kashimira.[^drhp-p66-67]

## Industry and market

The Indian infrastructure and construction sector contributes roughly 8% to India's GDP and is highly fragmented and working-capital intensive.[^drhp-p58] The company operates in a competitive industry with both organized and unorganized players.[^drhp-p76]

Sector growth is driven by government programs including the 100 Smart Cities Mission, Atal Mission for Urban Rejuvenation and Transformation (AMRUT), Make in India, and Power for All, as well as rising urbanization, industrialization, and disposable income.[^drhp-p58-59] India's infrastructure investment need is estimated at ₹50 trillion (US$777.73 billion) by 2022.[^drhp-p22] The logistics sector was valued at US$160 billion in 2017 and expected to grow at a CAGR of 10.5% to US$215 billion by 2020.[^drhp-p59]

The DRHP also acknowledges headwinds specific to the business: the slow pace of government projects, a potential working capital crunch affecting profitability, and changes in government policies.[^drhp-p76] These are not abstract risks — they are the daily reality of a company that depends on government tenders and government payment cycles.

## The offer and its objects

The issue is a fresh issue of 44,28,000 equity shares of ₹10 each. The issue price has not yet been set.[^drhp-p29] Because there is no offer-for-sale component, the entire proceeds go to the company.

The net proceeds are to be used primarily for working capital requirements — ₹1,130 lakhs (₹11.30 crore) is allocated for this purpose. The remaining amount is earmarked for general corporate purposes, but the specific figure is not disclosed in the filing.[^drhp-p48]

The company has not appointed any independent agency to appraise the project for which IPO funds are raised, and there is no monitoring agency for the deployment of funds — only the Audit Committee will monitor.[^drhp-p18] This is a disclosure the filing itself flags as a risk.

## Financial trends

A B Infrabuild's financials over the five reported fiscal years (FY2014 through FY2018) show a company whose revenue has been shrinking while its profit has been volatile.

Revenue from operations declined from ₹9,021.12 lakhs in FY2015 to ₹6,053.05 lakhs in FY2018, a drop of 33% over three years.[^drhp-p27] This is a sustained contraction, not a one-year blip. The company has been getting smaller.

Net profit after adjustments fell from ₹95.95 lakhs in FY2014 to just ₹15.86 lakhs in FY2017, a decline of 83%, and then jumped to ₹314.66 lakhs in FY2018, a nearly 20-fold increase from the prior year.[^drhp-p27] The filing does not explain what drove this spike, but the pattern suggests that profit in this business is lumpy, dependent on the timing of project completions and cost recoveries rather than steady operational performance.

EBITDA (profit before interest, depreciation, and tax) shows a similar pattern: ₹784.46 lakhs in FY2014, rising to ₹945.78 lakhs in FY2015, then declining to ₹676.19 lakhs in FY2017, before recovering to ₹928.80 lakhs in FY2018.[^drhp-p111] The FY2018 EBITDA recovery is consistent with the PAT spike, but the underlying revenue decline means margins improved sharply in that year — a combination that warrants scrutiny.

The balance sheet shows a company that has been funding itself increasingly through short-term debt. Long-term borrowings decreased sharply from ₹672.72 lakhs in FY2015 to ₹133.09 lakhs in FY2018, while short-term borrowings increased from ₹3,082.27 lakhs in FY2014 to ₹3,537.35 lakhs in FY2018.[^drhp-p26] Total assets grew from ₹7,062.34 lakhs to ₹8,232.15 lakhs over the same period, but the composition shifted toward short-term liabilities.[^drhp-p26]

The DRHP discloses that the company had negative cash flows in recent financial years, which could harm business and financial condition. It also notes that failure to recover trade receivables in a timely manner could affect financial health.[^drhp-p16] The company had ₹405.02 lakhs in unsecured loans as of March 31, 2018, which are repayable on demand — any demand from lenders for repayment could strain cash flow.[^drhp-p16]

## Valuation and peer comparison

The DRHP provides a "Comparison of Accounting Ratios with Peer Group Companies" but does not name the peer companies. The filing shows an EPS of 12.39 for one period and a P/E of 44.71 for the same period, but the exact year and peer details are missing.[^drhp-p55]

For its own company, the DRHP reports Earnings Per Share (EPS) based on weighted average number of shares: ₹1.31 for FY2016, ₹0.63 for FY2017, and ₹12.59 for FY2018. The corresponding Price/Earnings ratios are 4.14, 1.97, and 28.14 for the same periods.[^drhp-p54]

The issue price is to be determined and will be justified by the company in consultation with the lead manager based on these accounting ratios.[^drhp-p55]

## Ownership and governance

The sole promoter is Amit Bholanath Mishra, who holds 74,95,476 equity shares, representing 90.949% of the pre-issue capital. His PAN is AAMPM3121G.[^drhp-p100]

Other directors hold only token shares: Bharat Kumar Parmar, Shree Prakash Singh, and Mukesh Pandey each hold 300 equity shares.[^drhp-p93] Hatim Sakerwala, a key management personnel, holds 9% of the equity shares.[^drhp-p98]

Key management personnel include Ms. Anchal Pachori, aged 28, a Chartered Accountant who serves as Chief Financial Officer, and Mr. Mohit Soni, aged 26, an Associate Member of the Institute of Company Secretaries of India, who serves as Company Secretary and Compliance Officer.[^drhp-p97]

Related-party transactions include loans taken from KMP/Board of Directors amounting to ₹68.4 lakhs in FY2018, director remuneration of ₹57.7 lakhs, and outstanding payables to related parties of ₹324.3 lakhs as of March 31, 2018.[^drhp-p129-130]

The DRHP states that no director is on the RBI list of wilful defaulters and there are no criminal cases against directors.[^drhp-p90] Two companies promoted by the promoter — Al-Noor Chickens Private Limited and A B Magma Automobiles Private Limited — were struck off by the Registrar of Companies in March 2016 for non-operation of business.[^drhp-p18] The DRHP also notes that the company issued equity shares in the last 12 months at a price that may be lower than the issue price.[^drhp-p16]

## Key risks (top 5)

**1. Customer concentration on Indian Railways.** Indian Railways contributed 57% of revenue in Fiscal 2018, and Western Railway accounts for 52% of the orders on hand. Loss of this client, through contract cancellation, non-renewal, or a change in procurement policy, would cause a severe and immediate revenue drop.[^drhp-p12-65]

**2. Revenue dependence on winning government tenders.** The company's revenues rely entirely on being the lowest bidder in government tenders. If bids are not accepted or are negotiated to lower values, revenue would suffer. The company has no control over the volume or timing of tender awards.[^drhp-p12-72]

**3. Geographical concentration in Mumbai.** A major portion of revenue comes from operations in and around Mumbai. Any adverse development in that region — a natural disaster, a change in municipal policy, or a slowdown in local infrastructure spending — could disproportionately impact results.[^drhp-p13]

**4. Negative cash flows and high short-term debt.** The company had negative cash flows in recent years and short-term borrowings of ₹3,537.35 lakhs as of FY2018, with unsecured loans of ₹405.02 lakhs repayable on demand. Sustained negative cash flow could harm operations, and a demand for repayment of unsecured loans could create a liquidity crisis.[^drhp-p16-26]

**5. Related-party transactions and struck-off promoter companies.** The company has ongoing related-party transactions — loans, director remuneration, and outstanding payables of ₹324.3 lakhs.[^drhp-p15-130] Two companies promoted by the promoter were struck off by the ROC for non-operation, raising governance concerns about the promoter's business practices.[^drhp-p18]
