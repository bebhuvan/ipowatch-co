A B Infrabuild Limited is a construction company that builds railway platforms, bridges, and roads, and also operates a concrete plant. [^drhp-p63-71] The proceeds are earmarked primarily for working capital, a signal that the business needs cash to keep operating. [^drhp-p48] The promoter, Amit Bholanath Mishra, holds 90.949% of the company before the issue. [^drhp-p100]

The company operates in two segments: infrastructure creation and a Ready Mix Concrete (RMC) plant. [^drhp-p63] The infrastructure work covers railway platforms, station buildings, gauge conversion, track laying, and various types of bridges — beam, cantilever, arch, suspension, cable-stayed, and truss — as well as bituminous and concrete roads. [^drhp-p71] The RMC plant is located at Kashimira in Thane district, Maharashtra. [^drhp-p67]

Contracts are won through a standard tender process. A client floats a tender, a pre-bid meeting is held, the contractor submits a bid, and the lowest quote wins a Letter of Acceptance. The contractor then deposits a performance guarantee before the site is handed over for execution. [^drhp-p72]

## What the company does

The company's major clients are government bodies and state-owned enterprises: Western Railway, Central Railway, Mumbai Railway Vikas Corporation (MRVCL), the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM), and the Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority (MMRDA). [^drhp-p63] Western Railway alone contributed 52% of the orders on hand as of the filing date. [^drhp-p65] The company has worked with Western Railway since 2006. [^drhp-p67]

Revenue concentration is high. In FY2018, the company's top customers accounted for 57% of total revenue. [^drhp-p67] The DRHP lists this as a risk: loss of Indian Railways as a customer would cause a revenue dip. [^drhp-p12]

The company lists its competitive strengths as long-standing relationships with clients, use of modern techniques like box-pushing, experienced management with over 20 years in the industry, a modern facility, and a healthy order book. [^drhp-p67]

## Market and industry context

The infrastructure sector in India is fragmented and working capital intensive. It contributes approximately 8% to India's GDP at constant prices. [^drhp-p58] Demand drivers include government flagship programs — the 100 Smart Cities Mission, AMRUT, Make in India, and Power for All — [^drhp-p58] as well as eased FDI norms in 15 sectors including real estate and construction, and rising industrialization, urbanization, disposable income, and population growth. [^drhp-p59]

India requires investment of ₹50 trillion (US$777.73 billion) in infrastructure by 2022 to achieve sustainable development. [^drhp-p22] The logistics sector is expected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 10.5%, from US$160 billion in 2017 to US$215 billion by 2020. [^drhp-p59]

The filing acknowledges headwinds: the slow pace of government projects, working capital crunches, and changes in government policies. [^drhp-p76] Competition comes from both organized and unorganized sectors. [^drhp-p76] The market is fragmented. [^drhp-p58]

## The IPO: structure and use of proceeds

The issue comprises 44,28,000 equity shares of face value ₹10 each, at a price to be determined. [^drhp-p29]

The net proceeds are allocated to meet working capital requirements (₹1,130 lakhs) and general corporate purposes (amount not disclosed). [^drhp-p48] The company has not appointed an independent agency to appraise the proposed project, nor a monitoring agency to track fund deployment. Deployment is at the discretion of management and the board, subject to audit committee oversight. [^drhp-p18]

The lead manager to the issue is Mark Corporate Advisors Private Limited and the registrar is Bigshare Services Private Limited. [^drhp-p31]

## Financial performance

The company's revenue has declined over the five reported years. From ₹86.61 crore in FY2014, revenue rose to ₹90.21 crore in FY2015, then fell steadily to ₹74.53 crore in FY2016, ₹73.44 crore in FY2017, and ₹60.53 crore in FY2018. That is a drop of about 33% from the peak. [^drhp-p27]

Profit after tax (PAT) has been erratic. It stood at ₹0.96 crore in FY2014, ₹0.78 crore in FY2015, ₹0.33 crore in FY2016, and just ₹0.16 crore in FY2017, a steady decline. Then in FY2018, PAT jumped to ₹3.15 crore. [^drhp-p27] The filing does not explain what drove this spike.

EBITDA (profit before interest, depreciation, and tax) followed a similar pattern: ₹7.84 crore in FY2014, ₹9.46 crore in FY2015, ₹7.83 crore in FY2016, ₹6.76 crore in FY2017, and ₹9.29 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p111]

Debt levels have shifted. Long-term borrowings fell from ₹6.73 crore in FY2015 to ₹1.33 crore in FY2018. But short-term borrowings rose from ₹30.82 crore in FY2014 to ₹35.37 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p26] The company has also taken ₹405.02 lakhs in unsecured loans that are repayable on demand. A demand for repayment could strain cash flow. [^drhp-p16]

The DRHP flags that the company has experienced negative cash flow in recent financial years. [^drhp-p16] Sustained negative cash flow could impair the company's ability to fund operations and service debt.

## Governance and shareholding

The promoter and managing director is Amit Bholanath Mishra, who holds 74,95,476 equity shares, representing 90.949% of the pre-issue capital. [^drhp-p100] Mr. Mishra's PAN is AAMPM3121G. [^drhp-p100]

Two companies promoted by Mr. Mishra, Al-Noor Chickens Private Limited and A B Magma Automobiles Private Limited, were struck off by the Registrar of Companies in March 2016 due to non-operation of business. [^drhp-p18] This is a governance concern flagged in the DRHP.

Related party transactions include loans from key management personnel and board of directors amounting to ₹68.4 lakhs in FY2018, director remuneration of ₹57.7 lakhs, and outstanding payables to related parties of ₹324.3 lakhs as of March 31, 2018. [^drhp-p129-130]

None of the directors are on the RBI list of willful defaulters, and no criminal cases have been filed or investigations undertaken against any director. [^drhp-p90] But the DRHP discloses that the company, its promoter, and directors are involved in certain pending litigation, though specific cases and amounts are not detailed in the excerpts. [^drhp-p11]

The key management personnel include Ms. Anchal Pachori, Chief Financial Officer (aged 28, a Chartered Accountant), and Mr. Mohit Soni, Company Secretary and Compliance Officer (aged 26, an Associate Member of the Institute of Company Secretaries of India). [^drhp-p97] Mr. Hatim Sakerwala holds 9% of the equity shares of the company. [^drhp-p98]

## Material risks

**Revenue concentration on Indian Railways.** In FY2018, 57% of the company's revenue came from Indian Railways, primarily Western Railway. [^drhp-p12-67] Loss of this client would cause a revenue dip.

**Negative cash flows.** The company has experienced sustained negative cash flow in recent financial years, which could impair its ability to fund operations and service debt. [^drhp-p16]

**Unsecured loans repayable on demand.** As of March 31, 2018, the company had ₹405.02 lakhs in unsecured loans that are repayable on demand. A demand for repayment could strain cash flow. [^drhp-p16]

**Promoter's struck-off companies.** Two companies promoted by Amit Bholanath Mishra were struck off by the Registrar of Companies for non-operation, raising governance concerns. [^drhp-p18]

**No independent project appraisal.** The company has not engaged an independent agency to appraise the use of IPO proceeds, meaning investors rely solely on management's judgment for fund allocation. [^drhp-p18]
