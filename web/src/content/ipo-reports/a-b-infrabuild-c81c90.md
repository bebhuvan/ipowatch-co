A B Infrabuild builds the physical infrastructure that keeps Mumbai's trains running — railway platforms, bridges, station buildings, and the concrete roads that connect them. The company also operates a ready-mix concrete plant at Kashimira in Thane district, supplying the material that goes into those projects. [^drhp-p63-67] Now it is raising money from the public for the first time, through a fresh issue of 44,28,000 equity shares of ₹10 each at a price that has not yet been set. [^drhp-p29]

The company is tightly held. Promoter Amit Bholanath Mishra owns 74,95,476 equity shares, or 90.949% of the pre-issue capital. [^drhp-p100] The IPO will dilute his stake but leave him firmly in control. Mark Corporate Advisors Private Limited is the lead manager to the issue. [^drhp-p31]

## What the Company Does

A B Infrabuild operates in two segments: infrastructure creation and ready-mix concrete (RMC) manufacturing. [^drhp-p63] The infrastructure side covers railway work — designing and constructing platforms, station buildings, gauge conversion, and laying tracks — along with bridges of various types (beam, cantilever, arch, suspension, cable-stayed, and truss) and road construction including bituminous and concrete roads. [^drhp-p71] The RMC plant at Thane is a separate facility that supplies concrete for construction projects. [^drhp-p63]

Contracts are won through a competitive tender process that follows a standard pattern across government infrastructure work. The client floats a tender, a pre-bid meeting is held to resolve queries, contractors submit bids based on their eligibility, and the job is awarded to the lowest quote. The winning contractor then deposits a performance guarantee before receiving the drawings and site handover for execution. [^drhp-p72]

The company's client list reads like a who's who of Mumbai's public infrastructure agencies: Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai, Western Railway, Central Railway, Mumbai Railway Vikas Corporation Ltd, Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority, Dedicated Freight Corridor Corporation of India Ltd, and several private firms including Sky Way Infrastructure Private Limited, Dev Engineers, GCC Hotel Private Ltd, and Krypton Corp. [^drhp-p63]

This client base is not evenly balanced. Western Railway alone contributed 52% of the orders on hand as of the filing date. [^drhp-p65] The concentration is even sharper in revenue terms: its largest customers accounted for 57% of total revenue in the fiscal year ended March 2018. [^drhp-p67] The relationship with Western Railway dates back to 2006, which the company cites as evidence of long-standing client trust. [^drhp-p67]

<aside class="pull-quote">Western Railway alone contributed 52% of the orders on hand as of the filing date.</aside>

Geographically, the company is focused on Mumbai and Maharashtra, describing itself as "a major player in developing infrastructure in the city of Mumbai" that is "slowly consolidating our position in Maharashtra with a strong order book." [^drhp-p67]

The company lists several competitive strengths in its filing: quality and cost efficiency, transparency in dealings with stakeholders, modern construction techniques including the box-pushing method for executing projects, modern machinery and quality control systems, and an experienced professional team with over 20 years of experience in the infrastructure industry. [^drhp-p66-67]

## Industry and Market

The infrastructure and construction sector in India is highly fragmented and working capital intensive, contributing roughly 8% to the country's GDP at constant prices. [^drhp-p58] The logistics sector alone was valued at US$160 billion in 2017, growing at a compound annual rate of 10.5%. [^drhp-p59]

Several government programs are driving demand: the 100 Smart Cities Mission, Atal Mission for Urban Rejuvenation and Transformation (AMRUT), Make in India, and Power for All. [^drhp-p58] FDI norms in 15 sectors including real estate and construction development have been eased. [^drhp-p59] Rising industrialization, urbanization, increasing disposable income, and population growth are all cited as tailwinds. [^drhp-p59] India's working-age population is expected to increase to 64% by 2021, and the country is projected to be the world's third-largest middle-class consumer market by 2020, behind China and the US. [^drhp-p62]

The company also acknowledges headwinds: the slow pace of government projects, working capital crunches that can affect profitability, and changes in government policies. [^drhp-p76] Competition comes from both organized and unorganized players, making the market intensely competitive. [^drhp-p76]

## The Offer and Use of Proceeds

The IPO is structured as a fresh issue of 44,28,000 equity shares with a face value of ₹10 each. [^drhp-p29] The issue price has not been determined; it will be set by the company in consultation with the lead manager. [^drhp-p55] Bigshare Services Private Limited is the registrar to the issue. [^drhp-p31]

The company intends to use the net proceeds for two purposes. The first and primary use is meeting working capital requirements, for which ₹1,130 lakhs (₹11.30 crore) has been earmarked. [^drhp-p48] The second is general corporate purposes, for which the amount has not been disclosed. [^drhp-p48]

The company has not appointed any independent agency to appraise the project, and no monitoring agency has been appointed to track fund deployment. Instead, deployment will be monitored by the Audit Committee. [^drhp-p18]

## Financial Performance

A B Infrabuild's financials over the five reported years — FY2014 through FY2018 — tell a story of declining revenue, volatile profits, and rising short-term debt.

Revenue from operations peaked at ₹9,021.12 lakhs (₹90.21 crore) in FY2015 and then declined steadily to ₹6,053.05 lakhs (₹60.53 crore) in FY2018, a drop of about 33% over three years. [^drhp-p27] The intervening years show the trajectory: ₹7,452.76 lakhs in FY2016, ₹7,344.47 lakhs in FY2017, and then the sharper fall to ₹6,053.05 lakhs in FY2018. [^drhp-p27]

Net profit after adjustments has been erratic. It stood at ₹95.95 lakhs in FY2014, fell to ₹78.36 lakhs in FY2015, dropped further to ₹32.63 lakhs in FY2016, and hit a low of ₹15.86 lakhs in FY2017. Then came a sharp recovery to ₹314.66 lakhs in FY2018 — a nearly 20-fold jump from the previous year. [^drhp-p27] The filing does not explain what drove this recovery, but the swing is large enough to warrant scrutiny.

EBITDA — profit before interest, depreciation, and tax — ranged from ₹676.19 lakhs in FY2017 to ₹945.78 lakhs in FY2015, ending at ₹928.80 lakhs in FY2018. [^drhp-p111] The pattern is less volatile than net profit, suggesting that the variation in PAT is driven largely by interest costs, depreciation, or tax provisions rather than operating performance.

The balance sheet shows a shift in the company's debt profile. Long-term borrowings decreased from ₹586.63 lakhs in FY2014 to ₹133.09 lakhs in FY2018. [^drhp-p26] Short-term borrowings moved in the opposite direction, rising from ₹3,082.27 lakhs in FY2014 to ₹3,537.35 lakhs in FY2018. [^drhp-p26] This pattern — replacing long-term debt with short-term borrowing — increases refinancing risk, since short-term loans must be rolled over more frequently and are more sensitive to changes in credit conditions.

Total assets grew from ₹7,062.34 lakhs in FY2014 to ₹8,232.15 lakhs in FY2018, a modest increase of about 17% over five years. [^drhp-p26]

The company disclosed negative cash flows in recent financial years, warning that "sustained negative cash flow could adversely impact our business, financial condition and results of operations." [^drhp-p16] Specific cash flow figures are not provided in the excerpts, but the risk factor is explicit enough to be material.

The company also had ₹405.02 lakhs in unsecured loans as of March 31, 2018, which are repayable on demand. [^drhp-p16] A sudden repayment demand from lenders could strain cash flow.

## Governance and Shareholding

Amit Bholanath Mishra is the sole promoter of the company, holding 74,95,476 equity shares — 90.949% of the pre-issue capital. [^drhp-p100] He also serves as Managing Director. [^drhp-p93] The other directors hold only 300 shares each: Bharat Kumar Parmar, Shree Prakash Singh, and Mukesh Pandey each hold that token amount. [^drhp-p93]

Hatim Sakerwala, a key management personnel, holds 9% of the equity shares. [^drhp-p98] The other key management personnel are Ms. Anchal Pachori, aged 28, a Chartered Accountant who serves as Chief Financial Officer and heads the accounts and finance department, and Mr. Mohit Soni, aged 26, an Associate Member of the Institute of Company Secretaries of India who serves as Company Secretary and Compliance Officer. [^drhp-p97]

Related-party transactions are material. In FY2018, the company took loans of ₹68.4 lakhs. [^drhp-p129] Director remuneration amounted to ₹57.7 lakhs. [^drhp-p130] Outstanding payables to related parties stood at ₹324.3 lakhs as of March 31, 2018. [^drhp-p130] The filing acknowledges that the company has entered into related-party transactions in the past and may continue to do so in the future. [^drhp-p15]

Two companies promoted by the promoter — Al-Noor Chickens Private Limited and A B Magma Automobiles Private Limited — were struck off by the Registrar of Companies in March 2016 due to non-operation of business. [^drhp-p18] This does not directly affect A B Infrabuild, but it is a data point about the promoter's track record with other ventures.

None of the directors are on the RBI list of willful defaulters, and no criminal cases have been filed or investigations undertaken against any director. [^drhp-p90] The promoter has also confirmed he has not been declared a willful defaulter. [^drhp-p100]

## Key Risks

**Customer concentration on Indian Railways.** Indian Railways, the company's largest customer, contributed 57% of revenue in FY2018. [^drhp-p12] Losing this client — whether through a failed bid, a policy change, or a dispute — would cause a large and immediate drop in revenue. The company's relationship with Western Railway dates to 2006, which provides some stability, but a single-client dependency of this magnitude is a structural vulnerability. [^drhp-p67]

**Dependence on winning government tenders.** The company's revenue model is built on winning competitive bids for government and public-sector projects. The filing states plainly: "Our attempts to secure government and PSU projects may not always be successful. Our financial condition would be materially and adversely affected if we fail to obtain new contracts." [^drhp-p12] The slow pace of government projects, which the company itself identifies as a weakness, compounds this risk. [^drhp-p76]

**Negative cash flows and demand-repayable debt.** The company has experienced negative cash flows in recent financial years. [^drhp-p16] Combined with ₹405.02 lakhs in unsecured loans that are repayable on demand, the cash position is fragile. [^drhp-p16] A lender calling in those loans could force the company to scramble for liquidity, potentially disrupting operations or forcing costly refinancing.

**Pending litigation.** The company, its promoter, and its directors are involved in litigation that is pending at various stages. The filing warns that "any adverse decision in such proceedings may render us liable to liabilities and penalties and may adversely affect our business and results of operations." [^drhp-p11] The nature and scale of the litigation are not detailed in the excerpts, but the risk factor is specific enough to be material.

**No independent appraisal or monitoring of fund use.** The company has not appointed an independent agency to appraise the project for which IPO funds are being raised, nor has it appointed a monitoring agency to track deployment. [^drhp-p18] Fund deployment will be monitored by the Audit Committee, which is an internal body. This reduces the external oversight that investors might expect when their money is being used for a specific stated purpose.
