A B Infrabuild Limited builds the physical infrastructure that keeps Mumbai's railways running — platforms, station buildings, bridges, and railway tracks — and also manufactures ready-mix concrete (RMC) at a plant in Thane district. [^drhp-p63-71] The company is now raising capital through a fresh issue of 44.28 lakh equity shares, with the issue price yet to be determined. [^drhp-p29] The proceeds are earmarked primarily for working capital, a perennial need in a business where government clients pay slowly and contracts require upfront performance guarantees. [^drhp-p48]

The company's dependence on a single customer — Indian Railways, specifically Western Railway — is stark. In FY2018, 57% of total revenue came from key customers, with Western Railway alone accounting for 52% of the order book. [^drhp-p65-67] That concentration is both a competitive moat and a vulnerability.

## What the company does

A B Infrabuild operates in two distinct but related segments: infrastructure construction and ready-mix concrete manufacturing. [^drhp-p63] The construction side handles railway projects (designing and constructing platforms, station buildings, gauge conversion, laying tracks), bridge work (beam, cantilever, arch, suspension, cable-stayed, and truss bridges), and roadways (bituminous and concrete roads connecting highways and cities). [^drhp-p71] The RMC plant, located at Kashimira in Thane district, manufactures ready-mix concrete. [^drhp-p63-67]

Contracts are won through a competitive bidding process that follows a standard government procurement pattern. The client floats a tender, a pre-bid meeting resolves queries, contractors bid based on eligibility, and the job is awarded to the lowest quote. The winning contractor must deposit a performance guarantee before work begins. [^drhp-p72] The company uses the "Box Pushing" technique and has invested in modern machinery and quality control systems. [^drhp-p67]

The client list reads like a who's who of Mumbai's infrastructure agencies: Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM), Western Railway, Central Railway, Mumbai Railway Vikas Corporation Ltd (MRVCL), Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority (MMRDA), Dedicated Freight Corridor Corporation of India Ltd, and several private firms including Sky Way Infrastructure Private Limited, Dev Engineers, GCC Hotel Private Ltd, and Krypton Corp. [^drhp-p63] Western Railway has been a client since 2006. [^drhp-p67]

The company's competitive strengths, as disclosed in the filing, include cost efficiency, timely delivery, technical expertise, and integrity and transparency in dealings. [^drhp-p66] The company also highlights an experienced team with over 20 years in the industry and a focused presence in Mumbai and Maharashtra with a strong order book. [^drhp-p67]

## Market and competition

The Indian infrastructure sector contributes approximately 8% to the country's GDP. [^drhp-p58] The country has a requirement of investment worth ₹50 trillion (US$777.73 billion) in infrastructure by 2022 for sustainable development. [^drhp-p22] Foreign direct investment in the construction development sector from April 2000 to December 2017 stood at US$24.67 billion. [^drhp-p59]

Growth drivers include government flagship programmes such as the 100 Smart Cities Mission, Atal Mission for Urban Rejuvenation and Transformation (AMRUT), Make in India, and Power for All. [^drhp-p58] Eased FDI norms in 15 sectors including real estate and construction development, rising industrialisation and urbanisation, a rise in disposable income, and population growth are also expected to fuel demand. [^drhp-p59] India's working-age population is expected to increase to 64% by 2021. [^drhp-p62] The logistics sector is expected to grow at a CAGR of 10.5%, from US$160 billion in 2017 to US$215 billion by 2020. [^drhp-p59]

The market is highly fragmented and working-capital intensive. [^drhp-p58] It is competitive, with players in both the organised and unorganised sectors. [^drhp-p76] The company's own SWOT analysis identifies headwinds including the slow pace of government projects, working capital crunches that may affect profitability, and changes in government policies. [^drhp-p76]

## The IPO and its objectives

The issue comprises 44,28,000 equity shares of face value ₹10 each, at an issue price to be determined. [^drhp-p29] The lead manager is Mark Corporate Advisors Private Limited, and the registrar is Bigshare Services Private Limited. [^drhp-p31]

The net proceeds are allocated to two purposes. The first and primary use is meeting working capital requirements, for which ₹1,130 lakhs (₹11.30 crore) is earmarked. [^drhp-p48] The second is general corporate purposes, for which the amount is not disclosed (shown as [●] in the filing). [^drhp-p48]

The company has not appointed an independent agency to appraise the proposed project, nor has it appointed a monitoring agency for fund deployment. Instead, fund deployment will be monitored by the audit committee. [^drhp-p18]

## Financial trends

The restated financial statements cover five fiscal years: FY2014 through FY2018. [^drhp-p26-27] All figures below are converted from lakhs to crore for readability.

*Source: Restated financial statements, pages 26-27 and 111.* [^drhp-p26-111]

Revenue declined steadily from a peak of ₹90.21 crore in FY2015 to ₹60.53 crore in FY2018, a drop of nearly one-third over three years. [^drhp-p27] The trend is not uniform across all metrics. EBITDA, which measures operating profitability before interest and depreciation, fell from ₹9.46 crore in FY2015 to ₹6.76 crore in FY2017, then rebounded sharply to ₹9.29 crore in FY2018, almost matching the FY2015 peak despite much lower revenue. [^drhp-p111] This suggests the company improved its cost structure or project mix in the final year.

Net profit after adjustments tells a similar story of a dramatic FY2018 recovery. After falling from ₹0.96 crore in FY2014 to just ₹0.16 crore in FY2017 (a five-year low), profit surged to ₹3.15 crore in FY2018. [^drhp-p27] That is a nearly 20-fold increase from the prior year, driven by the EBITDA recovery and possibly by lower interest costs or other adjustments.

The balance sheet reveals a company under working capital pressure. Total assets grew from ₹70.62 crore in FY2014 to ₹82.32 crore in FY2018, but short-term borrowings rose steadily from ₹30.82 crore to ₹35.37 crore over the same period. [^drhp-p26] Long-term borrowings, by contrast, fell sharply from ₹6.73 crore in FY2015 to ₹1.33 crore in FY2018, suggesting the company shifted its debt mix toward shorter maturities. [^drhp-p26] As of March 31, 2018, the company had also availed ₹405.02 lakhs (₹4.05 crore) in unsecured loans repayable on demand. [^drhp-p16]

The company's own risk factors acknowledge negative cash flows in recent financial years, though the specific cash flow statement figures are not provided in the extracted facts. [^drhp-p16] The combination of declining revenue, rising short-term debt, and negative operating cash flows points to a business that has been funding its operations through borrowing rather than internally generated cash.

Key financial ratios improved markedly in FY2018. Earnings per share (EPS) rose from ₹1.31 in FY2016 to ₹12.59 in FY2018, while return on net worth (RoNW) increased from 4.14% to 28.14% over the same period. [^drhp-p54] The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio stood at 3.82 in FY2018. [^drhp-p54]

## Promoters, governance and shareholding

The sole promoter is Amit Bholanath Mishra, who holds 74,95,476 equity shares (90.949% of the pre-issue capital). [^drhp-p100] He also serves as the Managing Director. The other directors hold symbolic stakes: Bharat Kumar Parmar, Shree Prakash Singh, and Mukesh Pandey each hold 300 equity shares. [^drhp-p93]

Key management personnel include Anchal Pachori, the Chief Financial Officer, who is a Chartered Accountant and member of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India, and Mohit Soni, the Company Secretary and Compliance Officer, who is an Associate Member of the Institute of Company Secretaries of India. [^drhp-p97] Hatim Sakerwala holds 9% of the equity shares. [^drhp-p98]

Related party transactions are material. The company acknowledges it has entered into related party transactions in the past and may continue to do so. [^drhp-p15] In FY2018, loans taken from key management personnel and the board of directors amounted to ₹68.4 lakhs, director remuneration was ₹57.7 lakhs, and outstanding payables to related parties stood at ₹324.3 lakhs as of March 31, 2018. [^drhp-p129-130]

No criminal cases have been filed or investigations undertaken against any director, and none of the directors appear on the RBI list of willful defaulters. [^drhp-p90] The promoter has also confirmed he has not been declared a willful defaulter. [^drhp-p100]

But two companies promoted by Amit Bholanath Mishra (Al-Noor Chickens Private Limited and A B Magma Automobiles Private Limited) were struck off by the Registrar of Companies (ROC) on March 12, 2016 and March 11, 2016 respectively, due to non-operation of business. [^drhp-p18] This is a governance flag: it indicates that the promoter has previously incorporated companies that never became operational.

## Material risks

**1. Revenue concentration on Indian Railways.** In FY2018, 57% of the company's revenue came from Indian Railways, its largest customer. [^drhp-p12] Loss of this customer for any reason would cause a revenue dip and could threaten the company's viability. The relationship has lasted since 2006, but government contracts are subject to periodic re-bidding and policy changes.

**2. Dependence on winning government bids.** The company's revenues depend on acceptance of bids submitted to government departments. [^drhp-p12] If a majority of bids are not accepted or are awarded at lower values, the financial condition would be materially and adversely affected. The company cannot control the timing or volume of government tenders.

**3. Negative cash flows and working capital stress.** The company reports negative cash flows in recent financial years and has availed ₹405.02 lakhs in unsecured loans repayable on demand as of March 31, 2018. [^drhp-p16] Sustained negative cash flow could impair the company's ability to fund operations, bid for new contracts, or service debt. The rising short-term borrowings (from ₹30.82 crore in FY2014 to ₹35.37 crore in FY2018) reinforce this concern. [^drhp-p26]

**4. Related party transactions.** The company acknowledges it has entered into related party transactions in the past and may continue to do so. [^drhp-p15] Loans from key management personnel amounted to ₹68.4 lakhs in FY2018, and outstanding payables to related parties stood at ₹324.3 lakhs as of March 31, 2018. [^drhp-p129-130] These transactions may not be on arm's length terms, and the company's continued reliance on related party funding raises questions about its access to independent financing.

**5. Struck-off promoter companies.** Two companies promoted by Amit Bholanath Mishra (Al-Noor Chickens Private Limited and A B Magma Automobiles Private Limited) were struck off by the ROC for non-operation. [^drhp-p18] While this does not directly affect A B Infrabuild, it raises concerns about the promoter's track record in establishing and running businesses.
